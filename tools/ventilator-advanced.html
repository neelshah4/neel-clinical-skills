01Scope

What is assumed, and what this tool adds

This tool assumes ventilator fundamentals. Everything covered there is cross-linked rather than repeated: the single-compartment lung, compliance, resistance, the time constant, what volume control and pressure control each hold constant, triggering, rise time and cycle-off, the inspiratory hold and the expiratory hold, and reading the three scalars.

What is added here is the second pressure. Fundamentals treats the patient as passive, so the ventilator is the only pressure source. Every question in this tool arises once the patient generates pressure of their own: how Pmus and Pvent sum, why the same lung can be over-stretched at a normal airway pressure, why a mode that defends tidal volume hides deterioration, how PEEP is chosen when compliance is the target rather than oxygenation, and why a CO2 problem and an oxygenation problem are separate failures with separate levers. NAVA and ASV close the tool because each is an engineering answer to one of those problems: NAVA to the coupling problem, ASV to the settings-selection problem.

How the numbers are labelled

Identically to the other tools in this set. Every quantitative claim carries a PMID or DOI chip, or is labelled derived, consensus, extrapolated or author-chosen in place. Population is stated on every number, because most of this literature is adult and the audience is pediatric. Where only adult data exist, the tool says so rather than silently transporting the number.

02The equation

The equation of motion, with both pressure sources

Fundamentals gave the equation of motion for a passive patient. The full form has two pressure sources on the left:

Pvent + Pmus = VT/CRS + (Raw × inspiratory flow) + PEEP + PEEPi

Inertance is the only term dropped, and it is dropped because it is small. Hess 2014, PMID 25336536 Everything that follows in this tool is bookkeeping on that single line.

01

The airway pressure the ventilator displays is Pvent alone. It is one of two terms on the left, and it is the only one on the monitor. A patient generating 15 cmH2O of Pmus against a pressure-control setting of 15 cmH2O is distending the lung as though 30 cmH2O had been applied, and the ventilator shows 15.

02

The compliance on the screen is not a measurement of that patient's lung. The least-squares fitting ventilators use to display continuous compliance and resistance assumes Pmus = 0. Hess states the limitation directly: the method is less valid when the patient is actively breathing. PMID 25336536

03

The stretch that injures the lung is transpulmonary, not airway. Pmus lowers pleural pressure, so it raises transpulmonary pressure while lowering, or at best not raising, airway pressure. Intensive Care Med 2016, PMID 27334266

The pleural pressure change is not even uniform

Yoshida measured local pleural pressure with intrabronchial balloons in lung-injured pigs and found −13.0 ± 4.0 cmH2O in dependent regions against −6.4 ± 3.8 cmH2O in non-dependent regions during spontaneous effort, with pendelluft on both electrical impedance tomography and dynamic CT despite tidal volume held below 6 mL/kg. Producing the same dependent-lung inflation during paralysis required almost threefold greater driving pressure: 28.0 ± 0.5 versus 10.3 ± 0.6 cmH2O, and 14.8 ± 4.6 versus 5.8 ± 1.6 mL/kg. animal Yoshida 2013, PMID 24199628

That is the mechanism behind patient self-inflicted lung injury as framed by Brochard PMID 27626833, and it is why a protective tidal volume does not guarantee a protective breath. The rig below cannot show it. A single-compartment model has one pressure everywhere; pendelluft requires at least two compartments with different time constants. The rig shows the summation, not the regional consequence.

03Measuring

Two bedside measurements of inspiratory effort

Two bedside measurements convert the invisible term into a number, and both are occlusion maneuvers.

Whole-breath occlusion, ΔPocc

Occlude at end expiration and let the patient pull against the closed circuit for one breath. The airway pressure swing is a direct read on muscle pressure. Bertoni derived and cross-validated conversion factors in 16 ventilated adults over 52 daily recordings, with external validation in 12 more:

Pmus = k1 × ΔPocc   k1 = −0.74 (95% CI −0.78 to −0.69), simplified to −3/4
ΔPL,dyn = ΔPaw − k2 × ΔPocc   k2 = 0.66 (0.61 to 0.70), simplified to 2/3

The k1 interval is printed here in ascending order. The source prints it as −0.69 to −0.78, which is descending for a negative quantity and reads as a reversed interval; the bounds are unchanged.

Discrimination was good: AUROC 0.92 (0.83–0.97) for Pmus above 10 cmH2O and 0.93 (0.86–0.99) for ΔPL,dyn above 15 cmH2O, with AUROC at or above 0.94 in external validation. In that cohort Pmus exceeded 10 cmH2O on 84% of study days and ΔPL,dyn exceeded 15 cmH2O on 53%. adult Bertoni 2019, PMID 31694692

P0.1

The pressure drop in the first 100 ms of an occluded effort, before conscious modulation. In adults P0.1 above 3.5 cmH2O was 80% sensitive and 77% specific for high effort, defined as an esophageal pressure-time product of at least 200 cmH2O·s/min; P0.1 at or below 1.0 cmH2O was 100% sensitive and 92% specific for low effort at or below 50 cmH2O·s/min. Ventilator-displayed P0.1 tracked the reference well, but precision varied by ventilator and was worse in patients than on the bench, and ventilators that estimate P0.1 without an actual occlusion can underestimate it. adult AJRCCM 2020, PMID 32097569

The pediatric data

Rudolph studied 74 ventilated children, median age 3 months, over 118 measurements. Median first-breath proximal ΔPocc was 6.7 cmH2O (IQR 3.1–10.7) and P0.1 was 4.9 cmH2O (4.1–6.0). ΔPocc was unaffected by measurement site, proximal versus in-ventilator, and correlated with peak-to-trough esophageal pressure at r = 0.92 for the same breath. P0.1 agreement was much looser, limits of agreement −2.61 to 1.88 cmH2O, so P0.1 may be over- or underestimated depending on where it is measured. Both values rose significantly across the second and third breath of the same hold, so the number depends on which breath you read. pediatric Rudolph 2025, PMID 39945574

The practical rule. In children, ΔPocc is the more transportable of the two. Read it on the first breath, and do not treat a displayed P0.1 as interchangeable with a measured one.

04Targets

How much effort is right

There is a floor and a ceiling, and both are consensus targets rather than trial-derived thresholds.

Too little effort atrophies the diaphragm

In 191 ventilated adults with daily diaphragm ultrasound, thickness fell more than 10% in 78 patients (41%) by a median of day 4. Each 10% decrease carried an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.69 (0.54–0.87) for liberation from ventilation, an adjusted duration ratio of 1.71 (1.29–2.27) for ICU stay, and an adjusted odds ratio of 3.00 (1.34–6.72) for complications. Increased thickness, in 47 patients (24%), also predicted prolonged ventilation, adjusted duration ratio 1.38 (1.00–1.90). Decreasing thickness tracked abnormally low effort; increasing thickness tracked excessive effort. adult AJRCCM 2018, PMID 28930478

Too much effort injures the lung

By the pendelluft and transpulmonary mechanism in section 02.

07Weaning

Why the scaling direction matters for weaning, and for missing deterioration

A weaning assessment asks one question: how much of the work is the patient already doing? The mode decides whether the ventilator's own display answers that question or conceals it.

01

In volume control the answer is visible and free. Airway pressure falls as the patient contributes, so a scalloped inspiratory limb at fixed flow is a crude direct readout of Pmus. The plateau measured against real effort is not interpretable PMID 42273925, but the shape of the limb is.

02

In pressure control the answer is visible in the other currency. Rising tidal volume at fixed pressure is rising effort.

03

In adaptive pressure control neither number moves. Tidal volume is defended so it cannot report effort, and inspiratory pressure moves in the direction opposite to the clinical question — it falls as the patient does more, which reads on the chart as though the patient is improving. It is also what the ventilator does when the lung actually improves. The two are indistinguishable from the screen. PMID 17417981

04

The occlusion is the way out. It interrogates Pmus directly rather than inferring it. PMID 31694692 adult PMID 39945574 pediatric

The deterioration case is the mirror image, and worse

A child whose compliance is falling or whose dead space is rising must increase effort to hold minute ventilation. In adaptive pressure control the ventilator sees the volume being met and lowers its pressure; on the flowsheet, inspiratory pressure is trending down while the patient is decompensating. Jaber measured exactly this: assistance halved, from 12.7 to 6.7 cmH2O, when demand was raised. adult PMID 19225395 The flowsheet variable most people watch, peak inspiratory pressure, moves the wrong way in this mode.

What is not established. No trial has shown that mode choice changes weaning outcome in children. PALICC-2 could not make a recommendation on ventilator mode at all, calling it a research statement because there are no controlled-versus-assisted outcome data in pediatric ARDS. PMID 36661436 The one registered pediatric comparison of pressure control against PRVC for CO2 control, CoCO2, is a published protocol, not a result. PMID 39800404
08Levers

The separate determinants of CO2 clearance and of oxygenation

Tidal volume and dead space set CO2 clearance

Alveolar ventilation is minute ventilation minus the dead space contribution:

VA = (VT − VD) × rate    PaCO2 tracks VCO2 / VA

Both terms of the subtraction matter, which is why rate and tidal volume are not interchangeable ways of raising minute ventilation: at fixed tidal volume, every added breath adds a whole dead-space volume.

The cost of doing it with rate was measured. In 30 ventilated adults stepped from rate 15 to 33/min at a fixed tidal volume of 6 mL/kg, a 50% increase in minute ventilation (rate 17 to 25) lowered PaCO2 by only 5.1 mmHg (95% CI 4.2–5.9), systematically less than the alveolar ventilation equation predicts if dead space fraction were constant. It is not constant: alveolar dead space rose from 132 to 189 mL (p < 0.001) and dead space fraction from 0.60 to 0.65. Incremental CO2 elimination per litre of added minute ventilation fell as rate rose (beta −0.95 mL CO2/L per breath/min, 95% CI −1.41 to −0.49). Mechanical power rose exponentially: +181% with complete expiration and +122% at fixed I:E by rate 27. adult PMID 42306311

Raising the rate buys progressively less CO2 clearance for exponentially more power.

FiO2, mean airway pressure, and shunt fraction set oxygenation

FiO2 works on everything except shunt. Shunt fraction is the disease. Mean airway pressure is the recruitment lever, and it is computable from the settings. Hess gives both forms, and notes that mean alveolar pressure differs from mean airway pressure when inspiratory and expiratory resistance differ. PMID 25336536

constant flow, triangular:  mean Paw = 0.5 × (PIP − PEEP) × (TI/Ttot) + PEEP
pressure targeted, rectangular:  mean Paw = (PIP − PEEP) × (TI/Ttot) + PEEP
alveolar correction:  mean Palv = mean Paw + (expiratory flow / 60) × (RE − RI)

The rectangular form delivers exactly twice the pressure-time area above PEEP that the triangular form delivers for the same peak, PEEP and duty cycle. That is the whole mechanical argument for pressure-targeted breaths in hypoxemia, and it is an arithmetic identity rather than an assertion, which is why the rig prints both.

Mean airway pressure is also how oxygenation severity is graded in children: the oxygenation index, (FiO2 × mean airway pressure × 100) / PaO2, is the PALICC-2 severity metric, with the oxygen saturation index as its non-invasive analogue. PMID 36661420

The same variable is both the lever and part of the score. Raising mean airway pressure worsens the oxygenation index at an unchanged PaO2.

The cross-link back: every one of these three oxygenation levers is set through the same four numbers fundamentals already covered — PEEP, inspiratory pressure or tidal volume, inspiratory time, and rate.

09PEEP

Best-compliance PEEP and the decremental trial

Best-compliance PEEP asks a different question from the PEEP/FiO2 table. The table titrates PEEP to oxygenation. A decremental trial titrates it to the mechanics of the lung, on the argument that the PEEP giving the highest tidal compliance is the PEEP at which the largest number of units are open and none are yet overdistended.

Why the trial must be decremental

Hickling built a mathematical ARDS lung with gravitational superimposed pressure and explicit alveolar opening and closing pressures. During an incremental trial the PEEP giving maximum mean tidal pressure-volume slope did not coincide with open-lung PEEP, and it varied greatly with tidal volume and with lung mechanics: an incremental trial at low tidal volume tests recruitment by the peak pressure, not prevention of collapse by PEEP. During a decremental trial at low tidal volume, maximum slope occurred 2 to 3.5 cmH2O below open-lung PEEP unless closing pressure was high. The maximum slope was always much higher on the decremental limb — in the worked example 93.7 versus 16.69 mL/cmH2O — and the variation of slope with PEEP was greater, so the signal is larger and the peak is easier to find. mathematical model Hickling 2001, PMID 11208628

The animal validation

Suarez-Sipmann lavaged eight pigs, performed a recruitment maneuver confirmed by oxygenation, mechanics and CT, then reduced PEEP in 2 cmH2O steps every 10 minutes from 24 cmH2O in volume control at 6 mL/kg. Collapse, defined as the first measurable fall after the maximum dynamic compliance, occurred at PEEP 14 cmH2O. That coincided with the oxygenation-defined collapse point where shunt began to rise, and occurred one step before non-aerated tissue on CT exceeded 5%. Open-lung PEEP was therefore 16 cmH2O, one step above the compliance turning point. animal PMID 17110872

The rule as the animal experiment states it. animal In eight saline-lavaged pigs, open-lung PEEP was one step above the level at which compliance first fell. Read that as the derivation and not as an instruction. The largest human trial of best-compliance PEEP, ART, randomised 1010 adults to a recruitment maneuver plus compliance-titrated PEEP or to low PEEP, and 28-day mortality was higher in the titrated arm: 55.3% against 49.3%, HR 1.20 (1.01–1.42), p = 0.041. adult ART 2017, PMID 28973363 That trial randomised the titration and an aggressive stepwise recruitment maneuver together as one bundle, so it does not by itself refute a decremental trial run without the maneuver, although it does mean the number this rig finds has never been shown to help. PALICC-2 3.4.2, a strong recommendation at moderate certainty and the only strong one in its PEEP set, still puts pediatric PEEP at or above the ARDSNet table. pediatric PMID 36661420 No human trial has tested the one-step-above rule as a rule.

10Recruitability

Recruitment-to-inflation ratio, and the pressure an airway needs before it opens

Section 09 titrates PEEP by watching compliance move. This section asks the two questions that come before it: is there collapsed lung to reopen at all, and is the airway even open at the pressure being set. Both are answered at the bedside with the ventilator alone, and both came out of the same group in Toronto.

What the R/I ratio measures

PEEP does two things at once. It reopens collapsed units, which adds lung available for the next tidal breath, and it inflates units that were already open, which adds volume without adding surface. The recruitment-to-inflation ratio is the arithmetic of that split. It is the compliance of the lung PEEP recruited, divided by the compliance of the lung that was already being ventilated at the low PEEP, so a value near zero means the pressure bought inflation and almost nothing else, and a value at or above 0.5 marks a patient in whom the two were comparable. In the validation cohort of 45 adults with ARDS the ratio ran from 0 to 2.0 with a median of 0.5, and single-breath recruited volume tracked the multiple pressure-volume reference method closely, r = 0.798 with a bias of −21 mL. adult Chen 2020, PMID 31577153

Airway opening pressure comes first, because it changes every other number

In a lung with enough alveolar flooding or surfactant loss, small airways do not merely narrow at end expiration; they close completely, and the alveolus behind them is then sealed off from the circuit. Set PEEP no longer reaches the alveolus. The pressure that does is the airway opening pressure, the pressure at which the closed airways snap back open on the way up. Until that pressure is exceeded, PEEP is a number on a screen that the lung never sees, and every compliance, driving pressure and R/I ratio computed against PEEP is computed against the wrong denominator. adult Chen 2018, PMID 28557528

It is common enough to matter. Across 51 adults with ARDS pooled from two cohorts, complete airway closure was present in 41% (95% CI 28 to 55), rising from 22% in the lowest body-mass-index tercile to 65% in the highest, and driving pressure and elastance were overstated whenever the closure was ignored. adult Coudroy 2020, PMID 32701573 Surfactant depletion is the proposed mechanism, argued from lavage rather than shown directly. PMID 30560279

Deep sedation, and neuromuscular blockade in every protocol cited here. An inspiratory effort during the inflation invalidates the trace for the same reason it invalidates a plateau: there are two pressure sources again, and only one is on the screen.

Read the pressure-volume trace. An open airway takes volume from the first millilitre, so pressure and volume rise together from the origin. A closed airway takes almost none: pressure climbs against a sealed lung until the airways reopen, and the curve then bends sharply. The pressure at that bend is the airway opening pressure. Without the manoeuvre it is simply unknown, because nothing on the ventilator displays it.

04

If an opening pressure is found, set PEEP at or above it, and recompute driving pressure and compliance against it rather than against PEEP. That last result is the reason to bother. Half those infants would have been read as having a driving pressure over the PALICC-2 limit of 15 cmH2O, and the excess was an artefact of measuring against a pressure the lung was never exposed to.

The R/I manoeuvre releases PEEP by 10 cmH2O in one step and measures how much more gas comes out than the already-open lung can account for. The extra is what PEEP had been holding open.

11Two failures

Dead space and shunt are different failures

Both are ventilation-perfusion mismatch and they sit at opposite ends of the same axis. Shunt is perfusion without ventilation, V/Q approaching zero. Dead space is ventilation without perfusion, V/Q approaching infinity. The clinical separation follows from the dissociation curves, not from the anatomy.

Shunt

An oxygenation problem, and it resists FiO2. Blood leaving a shunt unit never contacts alveolar gas, so raising inspired oxygen raises the PaO2 of the ventilated units, whose haemoglobin is already nearly saturated, and adds almost nothing. Petersson states the rule directly: in contrast to other causes, hypoxaemia due to shunt responds poorly to supplemental oxygen. PMID 25063240

Dead space

A CO2 problem, and it usually presents as work, not as hypercapnia. CO2 dissociation is nearly linear over the physiological range, so an over-ventilated unit can carry away extra CO2 and compensate for an underventilated one. Oxygen has no such reserve: haemoglobin in the over-ventilated units is already almost fully loaded, so those units cannot make up the shortfall, which is why shunt resists FiO2 while wasted ventilation can be answered by breathing more. The commonest result of wasted ventilation is therefore increased minute ventilation and work of breathing rather than a raised PaCO2, because drive defends PaCO2 until it cannot. PMID 25063240

A rising dead space fraction in a spontaneously breathing child shows up first as tachypnea and effort, and only later as a number on a gas.

Benatar's iso-shunt lines are the graphical expression of the oxygen half and remain the reference construction PMID 4581075. Petros later showed the lines need modification at low FiO2: in 20 recovering ICU patients, the apparent venous admixture computed from the original diagram rose from 0.11 to 0.19 as FiO2 fell from 0.60 to 0.21, because relative V/Q mismatch contributes at low FiO2 in a way the pure-shunt model does not capture. adult PMID 8198900

12NAVA

NAVA: the problem it solves

The problem is coupling. Every conventional mode infers the patient's intent from gas movement — a pressure drop or a flow change at the airway. That inference fails whenever gas movement and neural effort are dissociated: auto-PEEP that must be overcome before flow starts, leak that mimics inspiratory flow, and a cycling criterion set for a different time constant than the patient has. NAVA replaces the inference with a measurement. Diaphragm electrical activity, Edi, is captured by electrodes on a nasogastric catheter, and inspiratory pressure is delivered as NAVA level × Edi, continuously, for as long as the Edi signal lasts. Sinderby 1999, PMID 10581089 PMID 35407471

How big the coupling problem is

In 50 adults across 7027 hours and 8.7 million breaths, asynchrony was present in every patient and every mode; median asynchrony index 3.41% (IQR 1.95–5.77), commonest event ineffective effort during expiration at 2.38% (1.36–3.61); patients above an index of 10% had higher ICU and hospital mortality. adult Blanch 2015, PMID 25693449

In children it is worse. In 45 ventilated children analysed over 57 651 breaths, 33% of breaths were asynchronous and asynchrony occurred in every patient; ineffective triggering accounted for 68%, delayed termination 19%, double triggering 4%. pediatric PMID 26914624 A later pediatric series found roughly one breath in three asynchronous, rising over time and highest on the day of extubation, with no correlation to comfort score and no association with prolonged ventilation. pediatric PMID 34054119 So in children asynchrony is common and its outcome significance is not established.

13ASV

ASV: the problem it solves

The problem is settings selection. Somebody must choose a tidal volume and a rate, and the pair is not free: for a given minute ventilation, small breaths fast means more dead-space waste, and large breaths slow means more elastic work. Otis showed in 1950 that there is a respiratory frequency that minimises the total mechanical work of breathing for a given alveolar ventilation, given the compliance, the resistance and the dead space. PMID 15436363 ASV computes that frequency from the measured expiratory time constant and the measured mechanics, derives the paired tidal volume from a clinician-set percentage of a minute-ventilation target, and then delivers pressure-control breaths to reach it, adjusting continuously. PMID 17846747 PMID 36484170

The formula is described here and is not displayed. Four papers state that ASV computes the rate and tidal-volume pair as a function of the Otis least-work formula, and none of them reproduces the formula with its constants in any text that could be retrieved. The implementation constants are proprietary. The curve in the rig below teaches the shape of the trade-off and is not the ASV algorithm.

The evidence that ASV picks disease-appropriate settings

In 243 patients over 1327 ventilator-days, in passive patients ASV delivered different pairs by underlying condition: in COPD 9.3 mL/kg predicted body weight (IQR 8.2–10.8) at 13 breaths/min (11–16), in acute lung injury or ARDS 7.6 mL/kg (6.7–8.8) at 18 breaths/min (16–22). In patients who were actively triggering, the pairs did not differ between COPD, ALI/ARDS and normal lungs. adult PMID 17846747 That is the limit: the differentiation the mode is sold on disappears in exactly the patients who are awake.

The evidence that it adapts to worsening mechanics

In 22 healthy women undergoing laparoscopy in Trendelenburg, pneumoperitoneum reduced respiratory compliance by 44.4% and raised airway resistance by 29.1%. With minute ventilation held constant, ASV automatically raised inspiratory pressure by 3.2 ± 0.9 cmH2O (+19%, p < 0.01), rate by 1.3 ± 0.5 breaths/min (+9%) and TI/Ttot by 43.3%. PaCO2 rose 13%, from 4.4 ± 0.6 to 5.0 ± 0.9 kPa (p < 0.01), and clinician intervention was needed in one patient. adult, anesthesia, healthy lungs PMID 19142087 This is the cleanest published demonstration of a mode responding to a defined compliance insult, and it is the anchor for the simulator in section 14.

01

Rising auto-PEEP as a second mechanical insult

Two patients are hidden inside this rule, and it works out differently for each. What applied PEEP buys in obstruction is a reduction in the work of triggering, and a paralysed patient does no triggering, so in the passive patient there is no benefit side to weigh at all; the mode-dependent plateau and tidal-volume behaviour described here is that passive case, while the percentage rule itself belongs to the spontaneously breathing patient. Marini's rule is that applied PEEP below the critical closing pressure, reasonably estimated at about 80% of the occlusion-measured auto-PEEP figure unverified, narrows the alveolar-to-airway pressure difference without a proportionate rise in lung volume, provided expiration is flow-limited. In flow-controlled volume-cycled ventilation, added PEEP has little effect on plateau until auto-PEEP is nearly counterbalanced, after which plateau rises in proportion to the PEEP increment. In pressure-targeted ventilation with the inspiratory pressure referenced to PEEP, added PEEP below auto-PEEP raises the effective driving pressure and therefore the tidal volume; once auto-PEEP is counterbalanced, further increments do not raise tidal volume and may lower it as the hyperinflated lung stiffens. adult review Marini 2011, PMID 21700908 The denominator is the weaker half of that rule: the expiratory-hold measurement that supplies the auto-PEEP number is invalid in the actively breathing patient the percentage is addressed to, and in asthma it can read falsely low, because units that close before end-expiration hide their pressure from the manometer. Where the percentage came from, how it was restated on the way into pediatric text, and what is wrong with the measurement it divides are worked through in asthma critical care.

Monitoring plateau in volume control, or tidal volume in pressure control, is how the clinician knows when the added PEEP is optimised. Paradoxical responses do occur and were documented in patients with airway obstruction under controlled ventilation PMID 16003057; the offsetting effect in COPD during acute ventilatory failure is the classic demonstration PMID 8420430, and the mode-by-mode framing is standard in obstructive-ventilation practice PMID 33169215.

On the 80% figure. The mode-dependent behaviour above is stated by Marini 2011 and is not in question. The specific 80% fraction could not be confirmed against that paper's full text: the abstract carries no numeric content and no open full text was reachable, so the figure is left attributed and flagged rather than dropped or restated as if verified. The adjacent classic demonstration in COPD reports the counterbalancing effect without this tool asserting a percentage for it PMID 8420430. Do not carry 80% to the bedside as a rule from this page.

What this tool does not establish

Stated in the same register as the disclaimer, and placed before the reference table rather than after it, so nothing above is read as stronger than it is.

01

Most of the evidence here is adult. The equation of motion, the occlusion-to-Pmus conversion factors, the effort target bands, the bench characterisation of adaptive pressure control, the decremental PEEP validation, the driving-pressure mediation analysis, the iso-shunt construction and every ASV outcome study were derived in adults or in animals. Where a pediatric number exists it is given and labelled; where it does not, the tool says so instead of transporting the adult value.

02

No mode has been shown to change a pediatric outcome. PALICC-2 explicitly declines to recommend a ventilator mode, calling it a research statement. PMID 36661436 The one registered pediatric trial comparing pressure control with PRVC for CO2 control is a protocol. PMID 39800404 NAVA in children has synchrony data and nothing reliable beyond it. PMID 40708902 ASV in children has one 26-patient crossover on driving pressure, one 15-patient weaning safety pilot and one 30-patient FiO2-controller crossover.

03

Best-compliance PEEP is physiologically motivated and, as a bundle with an aggressive recruitment maneuver, increased mortality in the largest trial of it. PMID 28973363 This tool teaches how the trial finds the number and simultaneously says the number has not been shown to help.

04

Four published models are refused rather than approximated. The Otis formula as implemented in ASV, the ventilator-internal scaling from Edi to delivered pressure in NAVA, the Benatar iso-shunt construction and the adaptive-pressure-control support curve are each described qualitatively and none is computed. Any remaining curve that depends on an unsourced constant is a labelled schematic with an author-chosen constant named on its face. The refusals are listed one by one in the reference section.

05

The simulator is a single-compartment model with one compliance and one resistance, as in fundamentals. It cannot show regional heterogeneity, pendelluft, or the dependent-region overstretch that the pig study measured — which is precisely the mechanism the effort sections describe. That limit is stated again in section 02, where the effort rigs live.
