Neurocritical Care: PRx, CPPopt & MAP Challenge

Interactive Teaching Module for ICU Fellows

Educational use only, not a clinical decision-making tool. This module teaches the physiology and the underlying evidence; it produces no patient-specific recommendation, and the challenge simulator runs on synthetic data. Defer to clinical judgment and local protocols. PRx and CPPopt implementations differ between devices and centers, so thresholds quoted here may not transfer to your monitor. Scope: adult severe TBI with an ICP monitor in situ. Every study cited on these tabs is a traumatic brain injury cohort, and SIBICC states it is designed for adult severe-TBI patients monitored with ICP monitors alone. The PRx cut-points, the CPPopt construct and the MAP challenge are not validated in aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage, spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage, post-cardiac-arrest brain injury, acute liver failure or CNS infection, where the ICP-to-cerebral-blood-volume coupling and the response to vasopressors differ. Pediatric use is extrapolation; see Pediatric Considerations. PMID 31659383
🧠 PRx: Definition
Pressure Reactivity Index (PRx) is a moving Pearson correlation coefficient between slow waves of arterial blood pressure (ABP) and intracranial pressure (ICP), computed over a 5-minute window from 30 consecutive 10-second averages (the ICM+ / COGiTATE convention). The original 1997 description used 40 consecutive 5-second averages, and both conventions still appear in the literature. It quantifies cerebrovascular pressure reactivity (a surrogate for cerebral autoregulation). PMID 37119323 · 9218290

✅ PRx < 0
Reactive vessels: ABP and ICP move in opposite directions. The outcome threshold sits stricter than the zero crossing: PRx above roughly +0.05 in adults, and above 0.00 in children, already separates favorable from unfavorable outcome. PMID 21964774 · 39996125
⚡ PRx 0 – 0.25
Not a benign band. Sustained PRx above 0.00 already marks the transition toward unfavorable outcome. In adults (553-patient TBI cohort), CPP below 70 mmHg together with PRx above 0.2–0.4, and ΔCPPopt below 0 together with PRx above 0.2–0.4, correlated with unfavorable outcome. That combined-insult band extends above this card's range and is adult data; it has no pediatric equivalent, and the validated pediatric cut-points are different (0.0 for favorable outcome, 0.5 for mortality). PMID 38587420 · 39996125
🚨 PRx > 0.3
Impaired. Pressure-passive circulation. Associated with worse outcomes, and counted as a cerebral insult alongside ICP >20, CPP <60 and ΔCPPopt <−5 mmHg. PMID 37653526
📐 PRx: Calculation
  1. ABP and ICP sampled as waveforms at high frequency. Published PRx work has used 60 Hz (Depreitere 2014 validation dataset); the frequently quoted ≥100 Hz minimum has no located published source and is not asserted here
  2. Averaged into 10-second epochs, removing cardiac and respiratory pulsation and isolating the slow waves of autoregulation (<0.05 Hz)
  3. Pearson correlation computed over 30 consecutive epochs (= 5-minute window)
  4. Recomputed continuously as the window slides. No published source specifies a PRx refresh interval; Aries 2012 updated the CPPopt trend every minute, so treat the update rate as an implementation setting rather than a protocol figure
  5. Software: ICM+ (Cambridge), or LAx (minute-by-minute low-frequency alternative)
Averaging window is itself a design choice with measurable consequences: reparameterizing the average to heartbeats rather than seconds removes a heart-rate-dependent error in PRx (PMID 40372858).
🎯 CPPopt: Definition
Optimal Cerebral Perfusion Pressure (CPPopt) is the patient-specific CPP at which cerebrovascular reactivity (PRx) is best preserved (the nadir of the U-shaped PRx-vs-CPP curve). First described by Steiner et al. (2002) and tested prospectively for feasibility and safety, not for outcome, in COGiTATE phase II (Tas et al., 2021). A U-shaped curve is identifiable in roughly two-thirds of monitoring time. A 2021 international Delphi process could not reach consensus on the accuracy, reliability or validation of any current cerebral autoregulation assessment method. Measured lower limits of autoregulation range 40–90 mmHg in adults and 20–55 mmHg in children, and the PRx-CPP relationship is asymmetric (it deteriorates more steeply below CPPopt than above it), so CPPopt should not be drawn at the center of a fixed plateau. 👤 Individualized CPP Targets
BTF recommends CPP 60–70 mmHg (Level IIB), and the guideline itself states that whether 60 or 70 is the minimum optimal threshold is unclear and may depend on the patient's autoregulatory status; the pediatric guideline target is 40–50 mmHg. Autoregulation is patient-specific, injury-specific and time-varying. Deviation from CPPopt is associated with worse outcome at far smaller magnitudes than the ±10 mmHg band often quoted (a band that traces to a 12-child preliminary series), and the relationship is asymmetric: ΔCPPopt below −5 mmHg sustained for 30 minutes already marks the transition to unfavorable outcome, while positive deviation is better tolerated. PMID 27654000 · 30830016 · 38587420 · 40399968 · 26978532
⚠️ Limitations
Yield depends on the algorithm: single-window PRx-based CPPopt was identifiable in only 44–55% of monitoring time, while multi-window weighted algorithms reach 80–94%. Requires ICP monitoring plus software. Noise and artifact affect PRx reliability. COGiTATE showed feasibility and safety but was not powered for outcome, and a 2025 secondary analysis found no difference in grand-mean PRx between the trial arms. PMID 22622398 · 28486883 · 34407385 · 39623160
🩺 MAP Challenge: SIBICC Algorithm (Tier 2)
The MAP Challenge is a bedside test of static pressure autoregulation (sPAR), incorporated in the SIBICC algorithm as a Tier 2 intervention. It uses a deliberate vasopressor-induced MAP increase to observe the ICP response, determining whether the brain's autoregulatory vessels are reactive or pressure-passive. PMID 31659383
An earlier version of this tool also attributed the MAP challenge to the BOOST-3 protocol. That attribution could not be substantiated and has been removed: BOOST-3 is a PbtO₂-versus-ICP comparative effectiveness trial whose published protocol describes tiered ICP and PbtO₂ targets and no MAP challenge (PMID 35273066; NCT03754114, active and not recruiting, primary completion 2027).
This is an adult tier label. SIBICC is an adult severe-TBI consensus algorithm. The 2019 pediatric severe-TBI algorithm contains no MAP challenge: its second tier is decompressive craniectomy, barbiturate infusion, late hypothermia, induced hyperventilation and hyperosmolar therapy, and the pediatric guideline CPP target is 40–50 mmHg. A fellow who maps “Tier 2” onto the pediatric tier structure will not find this test there. Performing it in a child is extrapolation, not guideline-directed care. PMID 30830015 · 30830016
✅ Positive Challenge (Intact sPAR)
MAP↑ 10 mmHg → ICP↓
Interpretation: Autoregulatory vasoconstriction is working. Consider maintaining a higher CPP target, weighing whether the benefit of the ICP decrease justifies the risks inherent to pharmacologically raising MAP.
An unchanged ICP is not a positive result. SIBICC's intact response is a decrease in ICP, produced by the vasoconstriction attendant to active autoregulation; disrupted sPAR presents as a sustained increase. A flat ICP is indeterminate and does not license raising the CPP target. PMID 31659383
🚨 Negative Challenge (Disrupted sPAR)
MAP↑ 10 mmHg → ICP↑
Interpretation: Pressure-passive. Raising MAP may worsen intracranial hypertension. Return the target MAP to baseline. A disrupted result also permits permissive CPP reduction back toward the 60 mmHg minimum SIBICC adopts from the BTF guidelines, rather than holding a higher CPP with escalating pressor. PMID 31659383
⚠️ Single-Challenge Scope
SIBICC describes one challenge: raise MAP by 10 mmHg, hold for up to 20 minutes, then return the MAP target. It does not describe a serial titration. Stepping MAP up repeatedly to map an autoregulatory curve at the bedside is an unvalidated research technique that sits outside this algorithm, and SIBICC lists routinely raising CPP above 90 mmHg among the ten treatments it recommends against.
An earlier version of this tool carried a sixth protocol step instructing repeat 10 mmHg increments to a CPP ceiling of 100 mmHg, presented under SIBICC's name. That step instructed the opposite of SIBICC's own do-not-do list and has been removed; step 6 now carries SIBICC's terminal instruction to return the MAP target. PMID 31659383
📈 Continuous Automated CPPopt Monitoring
Software like ICM+ continuously collects ABP and ICP data, calculates PRx over sliding windows, bins PRx values by CPP (typically 5 mmHg bins across a 40–120 mmHg range), and fits a second-order polynomial (U-shaped curve). The nadir is reported as CPPopt. The original 2012 algorithm used a single 4-hour moving window updated every minute; the current prospectively validated algorithm generates 36 PRx-CPP plots from past windows of increasing duration (2–8 h in 10-minute steps), weights them by goodness of fit, and smooths the result with a 2-hour exponentially weighted average filter. 📊 Yield & Reliability
CPPopt yield ranges from ~44–55% with single-window PRx to 80–94% with multi-window weighted algorithms; the fine-tuned prospective algorithm used in COGiTATE reports 80.5% (IQR 70–87.5). Depreitere LAx method: 95% yield. Fine-tuned algorithm (Beqiri 2023): improved stability with modestly lower yield. R²>0.2 threshold filters poor fits. PMID 22622398 · 24745709 · 28486883 · 37119323
🖥️ Practical Requirements
Requires a continuous arterial line and ICP monitor. Waveform PRx needs high-frequency sampling; published work has used 60 Hz and above. The LAx alternative works from minute-by-minute (~0.017 Hz) trend data off a standard bedside monitor, and UL-PRx has been derived from 5-minute averages (0.0033 Hz) across pediatric, adult and elderly cohorts. ICM+ itself is research software, but CPPopt is no longer confined to it: implementations now ship on commercial, regulatory-cleared multimodality neuromonitors, and whether those reproduce the validated ICM+ algorithm is an open question in the literature. PMID 24745709 · 38263241 · 38570411
🔬 COGiTATE Trial (2021)
Phase II feasibility RCT (N=60). CPPopt-guided therapy was feasible and safe, with no safety concerns and no power for clinical outcomes. The intervention arm held CPP within ±5 mmHg of the CPPopt target for 46.5% (IQR 41.2–58) of monitored time, above the pre-specified 36% feasibility benchmark (p<0.001), a comparison against a benchmark, not against the control arm. A 2025 secondary analysis found no difference in grand-mean PRx between the two arms. The successor trial, COGiTATE-II (NCT07287358), is registered for 60 patients across four Dutch centers with cumulative GFAP release as its primary endpoint; it is again not powered for clinical outcome and will not read out before 2028. PMID 34407385 · 39623160

🗺️ Clinical Decision Framework
Population: adult severe TBI with an ICP monitor in situ. This framework does not extend to SAH, spontaneous ICH, post-arrest brain injury or other non-traumatic brain injury, and its tier labels are adult labels.
Step 1: Place arterial line + ICP monitor (EVD or parenchymal bolt).
Step 2: If ICM+ or equivalent available → enable continuous PRx and CPPopt monitoring.
Step 3: If ICM+ unavailable → perform bedside MAP Challenge per SIBICC protocol (Tier 2).
Step 4: Interpret PRx: negative = intact CA → may augment CPP; positive = impaired → caution with MAP elevation.
Step 5: COGiTATE defined concordance as CPP within ±5 mmHg of CPPopt and reviewed targets 4-hourly; COGiTATE-II updates targets every 4 hours. A 2025 reappraisal argues the relationship around CPPopt is asymmetric and proposes treating CPPopt as a lower CPP threshold rather than the center of a symmetric band. PMID 34407385 · 40399968 · NCT07287358
🩺 When to Use MAP Challenge
• Elevated ICP refractory to Tier 1 (sedation, osmotherapy, CSF drainage)
• Low PbtO₂ with suspicion of inadequate perfusion
• CPPopt software suggests optimal CPP outside 60–70 range
• SIBICC Tier 2 consideration
🛡️ Safety Considerations
• A practitioner capable of interpreting the results and halting the study must perform the test
• SIBICC specifies no numeric ICP stopping threshold; halt on clinical judgment
• If the challenge is positive, judgment is required on whether the benefit of the ICP decrease justifies the risks inherent to pharmacologically raising MAP
• CPP 50–100 mmHg is a trial safety interlock, the range pre-specified in the adult COGiTATE-II protocol outside which that protocol stops titrating. It is not a bedside target and 100 is not a goal: SIBICC adopts 60 mmHg as its minimal CPP threshold and lists routinely raising CPP above 90 mmHg among the ten treatments it recommends against. The pediatric guideline target is 40–50 mmHg, so this interlock does not transfer to children
• Results may not be stable; sPAR status evolves over hours
• No concurrent care changes during the challenge, including sedation, analgesia and EVD drainage
• Return the MAP target afterwards: to baseline if sPAR is disrupted, to the new elevated target if intact
PMID 31659383 · NCT07287358
A BTF 4th-edition Level III caution against aggressive fluid and pressor support to hold CPP above 70 mmHg is widely quoted in this context. It could not be retrieved and verified at the time this card was written, so it is not asserted here. What is verified is SIBICC's own position: a 60 mmHg minimal CPP threshold, and a recommendation against routinely raising CPP above 90 mmHg.

👶 Pediatric Considerations
The MAP challenge on the previous tab is adult-derived. SIBICC is an adult consensus algorithm, and the 2019 pediatric algorithm's second tier contains no MAP challenge (decompressive craniectomy, barbiturate infusion, late hypothermia, induced hyperventilation, hyperosmolar therapy). Using it in a child is extrapolation. Age-appropriate CPP targets differ from adults, and the pediatric guideline target is 40–50 mmHg. Pediatric evidence is no longer limited to the 12-patient preliminary series (Young 2016). STARSHIP, a prospective multicenter study across 10 UK PICUs (135 children, 12-month GOS-E Peds), validated the outcome association of PRx in pediatric TBI, identifying critical thresholds of 0.5 for mortality and 0.0 for favorable outcome. Secondary STARSHIP analyses show that CPP below the autoregulatory lower limit is independently associated with worse outcome, and that the lower limit exceeded 50 mmHg for more than 45% of monitored time, frequently above the fixed guideline target. Cerebral autoregulation testing order sets with TCD-based methods exist at some centers (Vavilala et al.). Heart rate is a quantified source of PRx algorithmic error; the personalized PRx (pPRx) reparameterizes averaging to heartbeats and removes that sensitivity, with direct implications for pediatric use (Briggs et al., IEEE Trans Biomed Eng 2025).
