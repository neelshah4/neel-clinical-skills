Respiratory / ARDS

ARDS Inflammatory Subphenotypes

What the models can and cannot tell you. Latent class analysis, the classifier ladder, and heterogeneity of treatment effect, with the arithmetic shown and the transportability gap stated.

Educational tool — not for clinical decision-making. This is a teaching aid for reasoning about latent-class subphenotypes of ARDS. It is not a diagnostic device, is not FDA-cleared, and has not been prospectively validated. Scope: the entire heterogeneity-of-treatment-effect literature here is adult, and every treatment signal in it is a post hoc secondary analysis of a trial that was itself negative. No trial has ever randomized patients on subphenotype. In children, the adult signature transports poorly and no treatment effect has been established — the pediatric panel therefore displays classification only and shows no treatment recommendation of any kind. Several variables discussed here behave differently across the age range and are labelled where that matters: bicarbonate and vasopressor use, both of which are inputs, and creatinine, which is not an input to any equation this tool runs. Defer to clinical judgment, the treating team, and local protocols. Do not enter protected health information — all values stay in this browser session and are never saved or transmitted.

Latent class analysis asks whether the patients inside a checklist-defined syndrome fall into a small number of biologically coherent groups. Applied to ARDS across at least six randomized trial cohorts and several observational ones, it keeps returning the same two: a hypoinflammatory majority and a hyperinflammatory minority marked by higher IL-6, IL-8 and soluble TNF receptor-1, more vasopressor use, lower bicarbonate, lower protein C, and higher mortality — roughly double in the adult trials (28-day mortality 39% against 17% in HARP-2) and about six-fold in the pediatric cohort (13.8% against 2.2% in RESTORE/BALI). The finding is reproducible. What follows from it is not settled.

Three questions follow, and this tool keeps them apart: whether the class can be assigned at the bedside and with which published equation, whether it changes what a treatment does, and whether any of it holds in children. In order: sometimes, only in adults and only post hoc, and largely no.

01What a latent class model does

Latent class analysis is a finite mixture model: it treats the observed spread of biomarkers and vital signs as the sum of two or more unobserved distributions and fits them without looking at outcome. The classes are therefore not defined by who died, so the mortality gap that appears afterwards is a property of the biology rather than of the fitting procedure.

Two facts carry to the bedside. In every trial cohort of this lineage (ARMA, ALVEOLI, FACTT, SAILS, HARP-2, ROSE, and the pediatric RESTORE/BALI, CHOP and UCSF cohorts), a two-class model has beaten a one-class model, and a third class has not improved fit. And class membership is a probability rather than a label, which every classifier downstream inherits.

How well the classes separate, and what the two-class result is a claim about

Separation is good and not perfect. Calfee's two-class entropy was 0.78 in ARMA and 0.87 in ALVEOLI; the ARMA value sits below the 0.80 that the same table's footnote gives as the mark of a useful model; and Dahmer's was 0.908 in children, with average posterior class probabilities of 0.973 in both pediatric classes. The 0.89 often quoted for ARMA and ALVEOLI belongs to Delucchi's latent transition model, a different analysis in a different paper, and is not the figure either LCA reported.

The two-class result is also a claim about this lineage rather than about the field. The 2026 scoping review cited here covers sixty adult studies across clinical, biological, computational, imaging, omics and machine-learning domains, and reports reproducibility as strongest in the biological and computational frameworks and weakest in imaging and omics.

The negative control that makes the finding load-bearing

In ALVEOLI, subphenotype interacted with PEEP strategy for mortality (p=0.049), ventilator-free days (p=0.018) and organ-failure-free days (p=0.003). APACHE III, tested the same way in the same patients, interacted for none of them — “no significant interactions between APACHE score and PEEP strategy for the outcomes of mortality, organ failure free days or ventilator-free days (p=0.58–0.99 for interactions)”. Subphenotype is not a repackaged severity score.

Surfactant protein D is there deliberately: it marks alveolar type II epithelial injury and barely separates the classes. sRAGE is the type I epithelial marker, angiopoietin-2 endothelial, protein C and PAI-1 coagulation, the rest cytokines. sRAGE separates strongly, so epithelial injury is not absent, although the systemic inflammatory and endothelial rows dominate. These classes are not gradations of lung injury severity; they are systemic host-response states discovered in a lung cohort, which is why the same two reappear in sepsis without ARDS and why the pediatric authors caution that they may not be specific to ARDS at all.

The clinical correlates follow the biology. In the pediatric cohort, vasopressor use was 80% against 35%, sepsis was the ARDS trigger in 39% against 7%, and bronchiolitis in 6% against 28%. Severity spread across both: 32% mild, 39% moderate and 29% severe PARDS in the hypoinflammatory class, and 47% of the hyperinflammatory class mild or moderate rather than severe. For CRP and the routine markers a reader reaches for first, see section 05: none feeds any classifier model here, CRP was tested as a classifier and performed worse, and section 05 lets a reader enter a CRP value to compare it against the published class distributions instead. 03The classifier ladder and the implementability gate

Latent class analysis needs a full biomarker panel and a statistician, and everything since 2017 has tried to get the same answer from fewer inputs: full LCA, then a three- or four-variable logistic regression on research biomarkers, then a machine-learning model on routine clinical data. Accuracy against the LCA reference stays high all the way down. Implementability does not.

A published model is usable only if the coefficients, the transformation, and every constant that transformation needs are all in print. That is the gate this tool applies, and where most of the literature fails. Four models pass and are the only four this page computes with: the Sinha 2020 three- and four-variable models and the two ancillary variants in Dahmer 2022 Table 4. Every other model here is display-only, with its blocking reason in its own row.

The Yehya 2024 intercept-sign problem

Its four printed intercepts carry no sign, and read as printed the model would call every patient hyperinflammatory. This tool does not guess the missing signs.

Why the printed table cannot be the model that produced the printed performance

Table 3 prints four models whose intercepts read 16.262, 29.439, 23.285 and 39.756, all positive, alongside all-positive biomarker coefficients applied to log-transformed values that are themselves always positive. A logistic model of that shape returns a probability above 0.5 for every patient, so its specificity would be zero — against the AUROCs of 0.95 to 0.99 that the same table reports. The printed table cannot be the model that produced the printed performance, and this tool does not guess the missing signs.

Table 4 separately reports specificities of 0.90, 0.90, 0.91 and 0.93, its footnote stating that “test characteristics were determined using a cut-off of ≥0.5”, though those rows are drawn from online supplemental table 5 rather than from Table 3. The typeset PDF, the JATS XML and the PMC HTML all render the intercept cells identically, so the discrepancy is not an extraction artefact of one format.

04The implementable equations, written out

These are transcribed from the published tables and figures so a reader can check the tool's arithmetic against the source. All logarithms are natural, and all models return the probability of the hyperinflammatory class except Famous 2017, below.

The + 1 inside every logarithm is published rather than a convenience, and a model missing its transformation constant cannot be run at all, the whole of the case against Famous 2017. Dahmer 2022 states it in Methods: “As described for the adult ARDS algorithms, a value of 1 was added to the measured biomarker value prior to log transformation and use in the model”. That sentence licenses this page to compute with all four equations rather than only display them. PMID 34883088

The coefficients, and what the signs imply

Table 6 gives intercept 2.25, IL-8 −1.97, bicarbonate 1.71, sTNFr1 −1.71, with a footnote stating that all three predictors were log-transformed and z-scaled. Converting a raw IL-8 to the z score the model expects requires the mean and standard deviation of log IL-8 in FACTT, and those constants are not published. The equation is complete on the page and unusable in practice.

The signs also run opposite to every model above (higher IL-8 lowers the logit), implying the modeled outcome is subphenotype 1, the hypoinflammatory class. The paper does not state the coding, so the sign pattern is the only evidence and this tool labels that reading an inference rather than a published fact.

Which cut-point the verdict obeys
Both rules are drawn on every strip; this chooses which one the verdict word obeys. A published Youden cut-off applies only on the tab of the population it was derived in. On the other tab the strip still draws it and the row withholds the verdict rather than transporting the threshold.

The assay platform is part of the model

These coefficients are calibrated to the immunoassay of the cohort each was derived in, and absolute IL-6, IL-8 and sTNFr1 concentrations are not interchangeable between platforms. The cohorts behind this page did not share one. A systematic two-fold offset moves the logit by 1.65 to 1.83 units on the two ancillary models, 63% to 70% of the 2.6 logit units separating the pediatric Youden cut-off of 0.068 from the conventional 0.5 — enough on its own to carry a patient across either. No published conversion factor exists. This is the largest practical error source here, and it is invisible in every number the calculator prints.

The arithmetic behind 63–70%

Cytokine coefficients run about 1.3 to 1.5 per natural-log unit, so a two-fold offset on one analyte moves the logit by roughly 0.9 to 1.0, about 40% of the 2.6-unit gap. A platform change moves the whole panel rather than one analyte, and both ancillary models take two analytes off the same assay, so two offsets in the same direction add. With the section 04 coefficients: ln(2) × (0.9107 + 1.4711) = 1.65 for the IL-6 variant and ln(2) × (1.3013 + 1.3367) = 1.83 for the IL-8 variant. Derived Both products are computed on this page; neither is a figure any paper prints. Figure 4 shows the same arithmetic for a random assay error.

CRP — a live comparison, not a fifth model

No model on this page takes CRP, implementable or display-only, and neither does the prospectively validated PHIND assay. SAILS is the one trial cohort that measured CRP, and Sinha 2018 put it head to head against the routine variables that contribute most to class definition: CRP with bicarbonate and creatinine reached AUC 0.85 (Youden 0.41, sensitivity 0.76, specificity 0.82), against AUC 0.93 for the same pair with sTNFr1 in place of CRP, p<0.0001. That equation cannot be built here — the paper and its supplement print only the model's Wald z-statistics and p-values (Table S3A), never its coefficients, intercept or standard errors, so nothing licenses computing a probability from it (section 03). What the field below does instead is compare an entered value against the paper's published class means — a distribution comparison, never a classification. PMID 30291376

The rest of the bedside panel, and where CRP does appear

CRP is in one published latent class model: it was added as a class-defining variable to the extended SAILS LCA, where it changed nothing — the two-class solution held and 98% of patients kept their original assignment. The authors' own reasoning for why it classifies poorly is quoted above. PMID 30291376

The fuller COVID-19 figures: CRP did not separate the classifier-assigned subphenotypes at all, 231 mg/L (142–300) against 236 (152–300), p=0.6343, and 227 (142–300) against 254 (162–300), p=0.1131, by latent class (n=483, single health system). In the same table procalcitonin (0.6 [0.3–1.4] against 2.3 [0.7–7.0] ng/mL), ferritin, lactate dehydrogenase, D-dimer and lactate each did separate, all p≤0.0005. Those are the only subphenotype-stratified figures for procalcitonin, ferritin and lactate available anywhere, because that paper states these markers were not measured in the prior ARDS phenotyping cohorts — and they come from a COVID-19 population. PMID 34543591

The bedside markers that do carry signal are the organ-injury ones. Sinha 2018 reports the hyperinflammatory subphenotype with hyperbilirubinemia, higher creatinine and lower platelets, and the pediatric EHR classifier listed above selected mean INR, minimum lactate, minimum platelets, minimum absolute neutrophil count and mean conjugated bilirubin — that paper ranks its variables by a directionless gain measure and prints no per-variable direction, so none is asserted here. None of this feeds any equation this page computes, and none of it is a classifier.

Attach this to every pediatric result

In LEOPARDS, a prospective multicenter cohort of 500 children with ARDS, the adult three-term signature — IL-6, TNFR1 and bicarbonate — was applied prospectively, giving for 90-day mortality:

AUC 0.62 · sensitivity 26% · PPV 24% · 14% classified hyperinflammatory · cohort 90-day mortality 13.4%

None of the AUCs on the rows below measures that. They measure agreement with a latent class assignment — whether a short equation reproduces a long one — not accuracy against a child's outcome. PMID 42387347

Why the four rows are not validated by that number either

LEOPARDS tested IL-6, TNFR1 and bicarbonate together, the three terms PHIND used in adults. The four rows below run IL-6 with sTNFr1 and vasopressor use, IL-8 with sTNFr1 and vasopressor use, IL-8 with bicarbonate and protein C, and that last set plus vasopressor use. They are different ancillary models from the same family, so the figures above are the nearest prospective pediatric evidence on how a short adult inflammatory signature behaves in children rather than a validation of these four equations, none of which has been tested against a pediatric outcome.

The four AUCs also do not measure the same thing. The two ancillary rows carry 0.956 and 0.954, agreement with the pediatric latent class assignment in RESTORE/BALI. The two Sinha 2020 rows carry 0.94 and 0.95, agreement with the adult assignment in SAILS; those two have no pediatric performance data, which is why they show no test characteristics and withhold the class verdict on this tab.

No pediatric treatment recommendation is displayed

Pediatric heterogeneity of treatment effect is not established: the one randomized pediatric test showed no interaction, and the one observational signal runs opposite to the adult observational signal for the same drug class (section 08). This panel classifies and stops.

06What a cut-point costs

Every classifier here reports two operating points, and the gap between them is the practical problem. In SAILS the three-variable model at its Youden cut-off of 0.295 called 40% hyperinflammatory with 84% sensitivity and 87% specificity; at the conventional 0.5 that fraction fell to 31%, specificity rose to 95% and sensitivity fell to 74%. The four-variable model behaves the same way: 44% at its 0.301 Youden cut with 91% sensitivity, against 36% at 0.5 with 82%.

In children the same shift is far more violent. The adult IL-6 ancillary model applied to RESTORE/BALI at a 0.5 cut-off gave a sensitivity of 0.35 against a specificity of 1.00 — one hyperinflammatory child in three found, the rest missed. Re-deriving the cut-off in the pediatric data by the Youden index moved it to 0.068, and sensitivity rose to 0.89 with specificity 0.88. The AUC did not change: 0.956 either way.

A caveat belongs on every Youden number here: “Calculating the Youden Index from the derivation (in-sample) dataset may have led to an over-estimation of model accuracy.” These operating points are optimistic even in the population that produced them. PMID 31948926

AUC is a property of the model's ranking; sensitivity is a property of the threshold, and the threshold does not transport. A model imported with its cut-off attached is two decisions rather than one, and only the first was validated.

† The erratum note — read it before you use the FACTT row

Famous 2017 was published with the fluid-conservative and fluid-liberal labels exchanged, and corrected in 2018. The corrected reading is the counterintuitive one: in the hyperinflammatory class a conservative fluid strategy went with lower 90-day mortality, and in the hypoinflammatory class a liberal strategy did. The uncorrected text is still in wide circulation, including in the PubMed abstract record, so reading the arms off that abstract reverses the clinical conclusion.

What the correction changed, and which record carries which text

The 2018 correction (Am J Respir Crit Care Med 198(12):1590) states the terms were inadvertently exchanged, affecting Table 4, three Discussion sentences, four abstract words and supplementary Table E7, and that the online article was replaced. As of this build the PubMed abstract for PMID 27513822 still reads “26% with fluid-liberal strategy versus 18% with fluid-conservative”, while the publisher-replaced version in PubMed Central (PMC5328179) reads “26% with fluid-conservative strategy versus 18% with fluid-liberal”. The row above follows the corrected PMC version. Do not reason backwards from the parent trial to decide which way round the arms go — FACTT's primary endpoint was 60-day mortality and it was null (p=0.30).

A second correction, on different content

A second correction followed in 2019 (Am J Respir Crit Care Med 200(5):649, “Additional Corrections”), not about the arm labels: sTNFr1 and angiopoietin-2 values in the online supplement had not been corrected for assay dilution, and driving pressure was mislabelled as plateau pressure in supplement Tables E1 and E2 and on the x-axis of Figure 1. The authors judged that these did not affect the main results, and a corrected manuscript and supplement were posted. It matters here for one reason: the supplementary sTNFr1 distributions are the sort of constant a reader may use to try to rescue the z-scaled Table 6 model, and the pre-2019 versions are wrong by a dilution factor.

The sentence that governs the whole panel

Every row above is a secondary analysis of a trial designed to answer a different question, and (excepting the two observational rows, weaker still) every one of those trials was negative overall. No trial has randomized patients on subphenotype; a 2026 scoping review of sixty adult studies named prospective phenotype-stratified trials as the outstanding need. Until one is done, this table describes hypotheses rather than therapy.

Figure 6 — Interaction, not effect

Two lines that run in opposite directions mean the same treatment moves mortality opposite ways in the two classes. That is the claim. It has never been tested prospectively.

The blank panel is deliberate. The HARP-2 result in the sourced record is a within-class survival difference and its p value, not a pair of per-arm mortality percentages. Drawing a line there would require numbers no source establishes.

08Children: what transports, and what does not

Figure 7 — Two different AUCs

Same signature. Different question. The upper number asks whether a short equation reproduces a long one; the lower asks whether it says anything about a child.

The pediatric panel opens with the number that governs it. In LEOPARDS, a prospective multicenter cohort of 500 children with ARDS, the adult three-term signature — IL-6, TNFR1 and bicarbonate — gave an AUC of 0.62 for 90-day mortality, 26% sensitivity and a positive predictive value of 24%, classifying 14% of the children as hyperinflammatory, against a cohort 90-day mortality of 13.4%.

Those four numbers ride on every pediatric result this tool displays, in the result itself rather than a linked note, because of one easily-made error. The pediatric classifier AUCs of 0.95 and 0.96 in the Dahmer and Yehya papers are accuracy against a latent class assignment: whether a short equation reproduces a long one. The 0.62 is accuracy against the outcome, and only that is a claim about a child. Showing 0.95 without 0.62 beside it converts model agreement into implied prognosis, the most likely misuse of this tool.

The same study found pediatric-specific signal: two- and three-term models on CCL7 and IL-18, with or without TNFR1, reached AUROC 0.72–0.73 in a training set of 300 and 0.66 in a validation set of 200, with roughly 40% improved net reclassification. Their coefficients are not in the accessible record, so the tool names them and does not compute them.

Pediatric heterogeneity of treatment effect is not established

This tool displays no pediatric treatment recommendation of any kind. The evidence is two studies pointing in opposite directions. The one randomized pediatric test (the RESTORE targeted sedation protocol, examined by subphenotype in the Dahmer cohort) showed no interaction with either duration of ventilation or mortality.

The one observational pediatric signal, from the CHOP cohort, ran the opposite way to the adult observational steroid signal: methylprednisolone was associated with lower mortality in the hypoinflammatory class (adjusted HR 0.31, 95% CI 0.10–0.96) and higher mortality in the hyperinflammatory class (adjusted HR 3.13, 95% CI 1.36–7.21), interaction p=0.017; under inverse-probability treatment weighting the interaction weakened to p=0.083 (HR 0.22, 95% CI 0.06–0.80 and 1.93, 0.97–3.84). Two observational analyses of the same drug class disagreeing on direction is not a basis for treating a child differently.

On the 0.017 — the paper disagrees with itself

Its Results text gives the adjusted interaction as p=0.0017, while its Table 2 gives 0.017 for the same adjusted row. This tool prints the table value and names the discrepancy rather than choosing silently. The unadjusted pair from the same table is unambiguous: HR 0.61 (0.24–1.52) in the hypoinflammatory class against 2.46 (1.21–5.02) in the hyperinflammatory, interaction p=0.017. PMID 37813544

09The age traps in the inputs themselves

Three of the highest-weighted variables in these models change meaning across the pediatric age range, and the models were not built to notice.

Creatinine is among the most influential variables in the adult clinical classifier, alongside bicarbonate and vasopressor use, a ranking read off that paper's variable-importance figure rather than a printed table. It is also the most age-dependent input in the panel: 1.0 mg/dL is unremarkable in an adult and severe kidney injury in a toddler, so any adult model taking raw creatinine reads young children as less sick than they are. This tool implements none of those classifiers, although the trap matters because their EHR-embedded versions are the ones most likely to reach a PICU.

Bicarbonate is the most heavily weighted clinical variable per unit: at −0.2596 per mmol/L, a 6 mmol/L shift, about one standard deviation in the adult derivation cohort, moves the logit by 1.56. Across its interquartile range it is not the largest swing in either Sinha equation — Figure 8 puts IL-8 above it in both, and vasopressor use as well in the four-variable model — although it is the only one a routine blood gas already reports, and the only one whose normal range moves with age.

Vasopressor use carries the largest coefficient of any term in the ancillary models, 2.08 and 2.34, and records a decision rather than a measurement. The threshold at which a PICU starts an infusion is not the adult ICU's, and it has drifted across the period these cohorts span. A binary worth two logit units and set by local practice is a fragility rather than a feature.

Further limits on the pediatric derivation cohorts

Infants under 30 days are too sparsely represented to support any conclusion in that age group — Dahmer 2022's own reading: not absent, but too few for any claim. The cohorts were also assembled under three different case definitions across four cohorts, and mixing definitions mixes the populations the coefficients came from, including inside a single paper.

Which definition each cohort used

Yehya's CHOP cohort used the Berlin definition and enrolled 2014 to 2019; his UCSF validation cohort used the 1994 American-European Consensus Conference definition of acute lung injury and enrolled 2008 to 2017; Dahmer's applied PALICC oxygenation criteria within RESTORE and BALI; LEOPARDS used PARDS criteria. 10What the two-class model does not see

Two findings bound how much of a patient the hypo/hyper axis captures, and both matter more in a PICU than in the trials the axis came from.

Inflammasome activation runs on a separate axis. In SAILS and HARP-2, 33% of patients were discordant between an elevated plasma IL-18 (≥800 pg/mL) and their class assignment, and IL-18 correlated only modestly with the defining cytokines (r = 0.17–0.47). A group classified hypoinflammatory carried high IL-18 and high risk, with 60-day mortality 42% (OR 3.3, 95% CI 1.8–6.1, p<0.001) in SAILS and 27% (OR 2.1, 95% CI 1.2–3.8, p=0.009) in HARP-2. A patient called hypoinflammatory has been told something about IL-6, IL-8 and sTNFR1, and has not been told they are low-risk. PMID 37695136

Why the size of that group is not stated here

It depends on a denominator the source leaves open. The paper reads “a substantial subset of individuals classified as hypoinflammatory by LCA (14% of SAILS and 43% of HARP-2)”, without saying whether that is of the hypoinflammatory class or of the whole cohort, so no fraction is asserted.

Prior immune status drives class assignment and changes what the class means. In 1826 patients across two sepsis cohorts, haematologic malignancy was strongly associated with the hyperinflammatory class (OR 4.3, p<0.0001), surviving adjustment for pathogen, bacteraemia and illness severity; solid organ transplant was also associated (OR 1.6, p=0.02) although not after accounting for bacteraemia. Hyperinflammatory classification predicted worse survival in the haematologic-malignancy group but not in the solid-organ-transplant or solid-malignancy groups. Immunocompromised children are a large fraction of PICU ARDS, and this is the population in which the prognostic claim is least secure. PMID 41150892

Set against that, the classes are stable enough to act on. In ARMA and ALVEOLI, more than 94% of patients stayed in the same class from day 0 to day 3, in both the latent class and latent transition models. The transition matrix is not in the retrievable record, so no per-cell movement probabilities are printed. Two caveats from the same paper: the two-class evidence was weaker at day 3 than day 0 in ARMA, and class 2 showed progressive organ failure by day 3. Whatever the classes are, they are not a snapshot of a passing hour. PMID 29477989

11Current evidence and remaining gaps

Prospective identification is now solved as an engineering problem. In PHIND, 30 UK and Irish ICUs measured IL-6 and TNFR1 on a near-patient benchtop immunoanalyser with a run time of about an hour and combined them with an arterial bicarbonate to assign subphenotype in real time. Of 490 patients subphenotyped, 89 (18%) were hyperinflammatory and 401 (82%) hypoinflammatory. Sixty-day mortality was 51% (45 of 88) against 28% (111 of 398), risk ratio 1.8 (95% CI 1.4–2.4, p<0.0001), adjusted odds ratio 2.7 (95% CI 1.6–4.4, p=0.0002). PMID 41887245

That study answers the feasibility question and none of the therapeutic ones; it ran no intervention. It establishes that assay turnaround no longer stands between the classification and a randomization, which removes the reason the stratified trial has not been done.

The PHIND equation is not one of the four in this tool

PHIND used IL-6, TNFR1 and bicarbonate; the Sinha 2020 three-variable model implemented here uses IL-8, bicarbonate and protein C. They are different ancillary models from the same family, and the PHIND coefficients are not in the accessible record, so the tool names the study and does not run its equation. PMID 41887245

Three things would change what this tool is allowed to say: a trial allocating treatment by subphenotype rather than finding the interaction afterwards; a pediatric derivation and prospective validation with published coefficients and a pediatric-derived cut-point; and a pediatric interventional signal that survives randomization, which at present does not exist in either direction.

Until then the summary is short. The classes are real, reproducible and prognostic. In adults they may be predictive, on post hoc evidence from negative trials. In children they are neither established as predictive nor reliably transportable, and the tool says so on every result it prints.

How the sources and coefficients were verified
Every PMID and DOI here was resolved against PubMed E-utilities and PubMed Central, most recently on 2026-09-01, with first author, year and journal matched for each of the twenty primary sources. Retraction screening was run by erratum linkage as well as by publication type, because a publication-type screen alone cannot see an erratum published as its own record: that screen returns two errata, both to Famous 2017 — PMID 30547667 (2018, the exchanged fluid-arm labels) and PMID 31469324 (2019, supplementary assay-dilution and a driving-pressure mislabel). Both are in the reference table and the first is cited beside the finding it corrects. No retraction was found. Coefficients were transcribed from the table or figure named in each row and re-checked by forward computation against the published cohort medians. The published operating characteristics are reproduced as printed and are not recomputed here, because that would need patient-level data this page does not have. Numbers that no source establishes are labelled DERIVED, marked as not re-verifiable, or are absent.
