Part A

Pharmacology of pediatric critical asthma

AScope

Asthma is the most common chronic disease of childhood: over 4.6 million US children under 18, about 6.5% of the pediatric population Rogerson 2025, PMID 39348943. Most respond to two drugs; the ones who reach a PICU are the ones who did not, and the evidence for them is thin. Systemic corticosteroids and inhaled short-acting beta-2 agonists are the only two agents in this table with a first-line evidence position. Everything below that line is an adjunct chosen on a balance of efficacy, side effects and cost, with few head-to-head trials to guide the order. That is the state of the field rather than a gap in the table.

A1How to read it

How to read this table

  1. Pediatric dosing is shown first, in the left dose cell. The adult figure sits below it in smaller muted type, so the reader can see how far the pediatric number is from the adult one. Adult dosing is never the default here.
  2. Every dose carries its source. A dose with two citations means two sources published two different numbers; both are shown. Four agents disagree between sources (continuous albuterol, IV terbutaline, ketamine, and the aminophylline therapeutic range), so those cells run to several lines and repeat as cards in A3 rather than being averaged.
  3. This is not a dose calculator and must not become one. No weight input, no computed milligrams. The sibling tool albuterol-hfnc-delivery carries the delivery-dose caveats and is the place for anything device-dependent.
  4. The evidence column says what the trial population was, not whether the drug works. No difference in a 30-patient pilot is not a statement that the drug is ineffective. Each evidence cell names the design, the n and the population, in that order.

Discordant Trials in the 1990s showed no difference in hospital stay or respiratory score versus placebo, with more adverse events. Against that, a 163-child trial in children unresponsive to nebulised salbutamol found greater spirometric improvement at 6 h, higher saturation over the first 30 h, and 0 intubations in the aminophylline arm versus 5 in placebo Yung 1998. A head-to-head emergency-department trial found magnesium superior for respiratory score, saturation, hospitalization risk and treatment failure Kassisse 2021. A PICU trial found theophylline as effective as terbutaline and far cheaper Wheeler 2005. A systematic review of 14 randomized trials found a poor relationship between administered dose and symptom resolution, length of stay, or need for mechanical ventilation, and concluded that the currently recommended dosing regimens may not represent the optimum Cooney 2016. Which undercuts level-targeting as a strategy. How often it is actually reached for is a decision of geography rather than of physiology: across 39 US children's hospitals and 29,026 PICU asthma encounters, the proportion receiving aminophylline ranged from 0.0% to 39.5% between centers Rogerson 2024, PMID 37615529. Some units never give it and others give it to two children in five. Adult guidelines now recommend against it in an exacerbation while pediatric guidance does not; see A5.
Magnesiumintravenous sulfate Increases calcium uptake into the sarcoplasmic reticulum, relaxing smooth muscle; also anti-inflammatory, through inhibition of mast-cell degranulation and histamine release. Those are the two mechanisms this page's pediatric source names. The classical pharmacologic account adds two more that act on the same causal chain and are drawn in the D1 schematic above for that reason. Physiologic calcium antagonism, competing with calcium at voltage-gated channels so less calcium enters the myocyte, and reduced acetylcholine release from the cholinergic terminal, which is the same node ipratropium acts at from the receptor side. Neither of those two is stated by a source cited on this page not sourced here, and they are named rather than omitted because the calcium-antagonism step is what makes the magnesium arrow and the ipratropium arrow converge. Bolus: 25–75 mg/kg (maximum 2 g) over 20–60 min, may repeat every 6 h Rogerson 2025. Narrower published ranges: 25–50 mg/kg/dose (max 2 g) over 20–30 min JPPT 2013; 25–40 mg/kg/dose over 20–30 min Shein 2016. Infusion: 20–25 mg/kg/h (maximum 800–1,000 mg/h), serum magnesium goal 3–4 mg/dL Rogerson 2025. That is a target with no ceiling printed beside it, and no upper toxicity threshold was sourced in this build unsourced. So the stopping rule here is clinical rather than numeric, and it is the monitoring column: falling blood pressure, loss of deep tendon reflexes, and weakening respiratory effort in a child whose respiratory muscles are already the rate-limiting organ. Short high-dose alternative 50 mg/kg/h for 4 h Irazuzta 2016. Refractory scheme: 50 mg/kg bolus (max 2 g) over 20–30 min, then 25 mg/kg/h under 30 kg or 20 mg/kg/h over 30 kg, never exceeding 2 g/h JPPT 2013. One PICU report of prolonged infusion described a mean 18.4 ± 6.5 mg/kg/h for a mean 75.2 ± 74.9 h, with no comparison group. The report it came from could not be re-identified in this repair round, so the figures are shown with their provenance stated rather than dropped or attached to a guess unsourced. Treat them as one centre's practice, not as a dose. Adult: a single infusion of 1.2 g or 2 g over 15–30 min Kew 2014. This is an adult figure and must not be read as a pediatric one. Rapid, within minutes. Hypotension. Specifically diastolic hypotension, the most common problem with prolonged infusion, especially when a high serum level is targeted, and usually treatable with fluid boluses. Also nausea, vomiting, flushing, muscle weakness. In a systematic review of continuous infusions the clinically significant events were hypotension 16.6% (n=74), nausea or vomiting 7.8% (n=35), mild muscle weakness 4.9% (n=22), flushing 2.2% (n=10), sedation 0.4% (n=2) Johnson 2022. Blood pressure through the infusion, serum magnesium, deep tendon reflexes, respiratory effort. Muscle weakness in a child whose respiratory muscles are already the rate-limiting organ is not a minor side effect. Supported in the ED, unproven after admission The strongest of the adjuncts, and still thin. Pediatric emergency-department Cochrane review: IV magnesium may reduce the need for hospital admission in moderate-to-severe exacerbations, but the evidence is extremely limited by the number and size of studies, five randomized trials and 182 children in total, with the admission result resting on three trials and 115 children (OR 0.32, 95% CI 0.14–0.74) Griffiths 2016. Adult emergency-department Cochrane review: a single 1.2 g or 2 g infusion reduces admissions and improves lung function in adults who have not responded sufficiently to oxygen, nebulized SABA and IV corticosteroids Kew 2014. Head-to-head, magnesium beat aminophylline Kassisse 2021. High-dose prolonged infusion sped discharge and cost 27.7% less per patient than the standard 50 mg/kg bolus it was compared against ($603.16 ± 338.47 against $834.37 ± 306.73, p<0.016) Irazuzta 2016. The comparator matters: the saving is against another magnesium regimen rather than against withholding magnesium. Retrospective PICU and ward studies failed to show benefit, most plausibly because sicker children get magnesium. Confounding by indication running the wrong way. The systematic review of infusions concluded that until better research exists, prolonged infusions should be reserved for refractory asthma Johnson 2022. It is the most commonly used IV adjunct in PICUs, on cost and safety profile.
Corticosteroidssystemic The only agent in this table that treats the disease rather than the symptom. It reduces cellular production of the chemokines and cytokines that recruit and activate eosinophils, mast cells and T cells in the airway. Give it early precisely because it will not help in the first hour. Hypertension, gastritis, hyperglycemia, adrenal suppression, agitation. Glucose, blood pressure, mood and sleep. Gastrointestinal prophylaxis per local practice. First line and untestable against placebo. Because giving corticosteroids to a child with a severe exacerbation is universal practice, there are no placebo-controlled trials in critical asthma; trials in this population compare drug and dose instead. Ketamineintravenous NMDA receptor antagonism. Activation of airway NMDA receptors releases sensory neuropeptides that drive smooth-muscle contraction; ketamine blocks that, and additionally disrupts histamine-mediated bronchoconstriction. Bronchodilator tier, non-intubated: 0.2–0.5 mg/kg bolus over 20 min, then 0.5–0.6 mg/kg/h for 2–3 h Rogerson 2025. The single randomized trial used 0.2 mg/kg IV over 1–2 min then 0.5 mg/kg/h for 2 h Jat 2012. Anesthetic / intubation tier: bolus 1–2 mg/kg then infusion 1–2 mg/kg/h Shein 2016; or 2 mg/kg load then 20–60 mcg/kg/min JPPT 2013. These are two tiers answering two questions, not one wide range. Presenting them as one invites giving an anesthetic dose to a patient who still has to protect their own airway. Seconds to minutes. A4Overlap

Toxicity that overlaps between agents

Read the adverse-effect column down rather than across. Tachycardia, diastolic hypotension and hypokalemia appear in the beta-2 agonist rows, in the magnesium row, and again in the methylxanthine row, and a child in a PICU is frequently receiving all three at once. The pediatric source says so directly: adverse effects of high-dose inhaled beta-2 agonists are to be expected, especially with concurrent intravenous magnesium, intravenous beta-2 agonists and aminophylline Rogerson 2025. A5Order

Which adjunct to start first

Which intravenous adjunct to start (magnesium, aminophylline, or an intravenous beta-2 agonist) and when to add the next is a balance of efficacy, side effects and cost, with few head-to-head studies. In the emergency department, magnesium beat aminophylline for respiratory score, oxygen saturation, hospitalization risk and treatment failure Kassisse 2021. On the ward, intravenous salbutamol improved respiratory rate faster than magnesium at one hour but caused more hypokalemia, and neither shortened PICU or hospital stay. In the PICU, theophylline was as effective as terbutaline, at about $280 per patient against $3,908 for terbutaline and $4,045 for both, with more nausea in the combination arm Wheeler 2005. Magnesium is the most commonly used PICU adjunct on cost and safety grounds, which is a statement about practice rather than about superiority.

Adult guidelines now recommend against aminophylline in an exacerbation; the pediatric evidence does not read the same way. GINA's 2026 report heads the section Aminophylline and theophylline (not recommended) and states that intravenous aminophylline and theophylline should not be used in the management of asthma exacerbations, citing poor efficacy and safety profile and the greater effectiveness and relative safety of short-acting beta-2 agonists. That chapter covers adults, adolescents and children 6–11 years; the separate chapter for children five and under says nothing about the drug either way GINA 2026, p.189. The 2007 NAEPP expert panel report lists methylxanthines among the treatments not recommended for emergency-department and hospital management of exacerbations, at Evidence A, in adults and children alike EPR-3 2007, Section 5, and the 2020 NAEPP focused updates did not revisit them Cloutier 2020, PMID 33280709. SIGN 158 is milder for adults: intravenous aminophylline is not likely to result in any additional bronchodilation compared with inhaled bronchodilators and steroids, arrhythmias and vomiting are increased (evidence level 1++), and it should be used only after consultation with senior medical staff SIGN 158, 2024. The ERS/ATS severe-asthma guideline makes no acute-exacerbation recommendation on methylxanthines at all.

The justification is efficacy against toxicity, and the trials behind it are adult. Across 17 randomized emergency-department trials in adults, adding intravenous aminophylline to inhaled beta-2 agonists changed no measure of airflow at any time point, while palpitations or arrhythmias rose (OR 3.02, 95% CI 1.15–7.90; 6 studies, n = 249) and vomiting rose (OR 4.21, 95% CI 2.20–8.07; 7 studies, n = 321). No subgroup in which it worked was identified, and the review concluded that the risk–benefit balance is unfavourable Nair 2012, PMID 23235591. That is the review GINA cites.

The pediatric Cochrane review points elsewhere, which is why the adult recommendation should not be carried into a PICU unexamined. In 7 trials of 380 children over two years old, all requiring admission and most already unresponsive to nebulised beta-2 agonists, aminophylline improved FEV1 % predicted at 6–8 h, 12–18 h and 24 h, with no significant difference in length of hospital stay, symptoms, frequency of nebulisations or mechanical ventilation rates, a three-fold increase in vomiting, and no significant difference in hypokalaemia, headaches, tremor, seizures, arrhythmias or deaths Mitra 2005, PMID 15846615. SIGN 158 splits its pediatric recommendation on the same line: Grade A, not recommended in children with mild to moderate acute asthma; Grade A, consider it in children with severe or life-threatening asthma unresponsive to maximal doses of bronchodilators and steroids, at a 5 mg/kg load over 20 minutes with ECG monitoring, then 1 mg/kg/h SIGN 158, 2024. In children the drug buys lung function that does not convert into a shorter stay or less ventilation and costs vomiting; in adults it buys nothing measurable and costs arrhythmias.

How much this uncertainty costs, measured. Across 39 children's hospitals and 29,026 PICU encounters for asthma, the number of adjunctive therapies used per encounter ranged from a center mean of 0.6 to 2.5 (p<0.01), the proportion of asthma admissions that resulted in PICU admission ranged from 5.2% to 47.3%, and center-level median hospital stay ranged from 1 day (IQR 1–3) to 4 days (IQR 3–6). The number of adjuncts a center used was not associated with hospital length of stay (p=0.81) or with the proportion of encounters admitted to a PICU (p=0.47). Rogerson 2024, PMID 37615529

That last result is administrative data with no severity adjustment, so it is not evidence that adjuncts do not work. It is evidence that the variation is large enough to be worth resolving.

Part B

Why CPAP and BiPAP work

BScope

Noninvasive support does not reverse bronchoconstriction. It changes the pressure the child has to generate to move the same breath.

Miller and Rotta state it directly: noninvasive respiratory support does not directly reverse bronchoconstriction but provides support to reduce work of breathing through several mechanisms Miller 2025, PMID 39362757. Three are named in that pediatric source, and this section follows those three rather than inventing others: augmenting tidal volume, reducing inspiratory work from intrinsic PEEP, and increasing the cross-sectional area of small airways throughout the respiratory cycle. This is not a device-selection guide and not an escalation algorithm; Miller and Rotta already supply those.

B1The model

The bag and the tube

The single-compartment model reduces the child to one elastic bag (the lung, compliance C) on the end of one resistive tube (the airway, resistance R). Everything CPAP and BiPAP do is a change to one term in the equation describing that bag.

Pvent + Pmus = VT/CRS + Raw × V̇I + max(PEEP, PEEPi)

Colour is consistent across the three figures in this part: elastic becomes the elastic area on the Campbell diagram, resistive the resistive lens, intrinsic PEEP the threshold segment, and set PEEP the EPAP slider. The equation of motion is Hess's Hess 2014, PMID 25336536; inertance is in the full equation and is disregarded because its effect is small. The baseline term is a maximum rather than two terms added. Under the absolute-intrinsic-PEEP convention used throughout, applied PEEP below intrinsic PEEP in a flow-limited patient offsets the baseline load rather than adding to it, so only the larger is paid. That is what the Campbell code computes (threshold area = max(0, PEEPi − EPAP)) and what every PEEP argument in Part C rests on. Added instead of maximised, 5 cmH2O of EPAP on a child with intrinsic PEEP 8 would read as a 13 cmH2O baseline load. It is 8.

The right-hand side is the bill; Pmus is the child's share and Pvent is what the machine pays, so every noninvasive knob raises Pvent to let Pmus fall at unchanged tidal volume. The elastic cost of stretching the bag; the resistive cost of pushing gas down the tube, which asthma inflates; intrinsic PEEP, a toll paid before any gas moves, which dynamic hyperinflation creates; and set PEEP, the only term you control directly and the one that offsets the toll.

Intrinsic PEEP exists because of the time constant. τ = R × C, and a lung unit empties 63% of the way in 1τ, 87% in 2τ, 95% in 3τ, 98% in 4τ and 99% in 5τ. Raise R and the unit needs more time; give it less than it needs and it starts the next breath still full, which is why the Campbell diagram in B4 has a horizontal foot.

B2The swing

Pleural pressure is the readout of effort

Pmus cannot be seen, although its cost can: every inspiratory effort drags pleural pressure down, measurable at the bedside as esophageal pressure. Once the balloon is correctly positioned it permits accurate measurement of transmural vascular pressure and of intrinsic PEEP, and allows titration of assisted ventilation to a chosen level of patient effort Intensive Care Med 2016, PMID 27334266.

  1. The swing. The tidal excursion of esophageal pressure is the crudest and most useful index of effort, and it shrinks visibly when CPAP unloads a patient: in infants with severe RSV bronchiolitis, nasal CPAP reduced esophageal pressure swings by 54 ± 4% and the inspiratory esophageal pressure–time product by 59 ± 5% Cambonie 2008. The CPAP level that produced those numbers is not stated in the verified abstract, so it is not printed here.
  2. The pressure–time product. Area under the pressure trace over time rather than its depth alone. It tracks the oxygen cost of breathing better than the swing because it counts how long the muscle is loaded as well as how hard, and it is the outcome measure in every unloading study cited here.
  3. The interval before flow. The part of the pressure fall that happens while flow is still zero. This is the threshold load, and it is the subject of B3. Appendini's operational definition of dynamic intrinsic PEEP is exactly this: the decrease in pleural pressure preceding inspiration Appendini 1994.

The caveat that travels with the tracing. If the child is actively expiring, relaxation of the abdominal muscles contributes to the pleural pressure drop and dynamic intrinsic PEEP is overestimated; this was true in almost all instances in Appendini's series Appendini 1994, and a validation study used the Campbell diagram as the reference standard to judge the esophageal estimate against it Zakynthinos 2000. A screaming, splinting child in status asthmaticus is the patient in whom this error is largest.

B3The toll

The inspiratory threshold load

When a lung has not finished emptying, alveolar pressure at end-expiration is still above the pressure at the airway opening, so before one millilitre of gas can enter the child must generate an effort large enough to erase that difference. The work done in that interval buys no volume. The naming is Petrof's: the presence of intrinsic PEEP acts as an inspiratory threshold load, and contributes significantly to the observed increase in work and oxygen cost of breathing Petrof 1990.

Marini gives the second half of the mechanism, which matters more in a hyperinflated child than the toll itself. Dynamic hyperinflation increases inspiratory work for two reasons. Initiation of inflow requires inspiratory force to counterbalance auto-PEEP, and during spontaneous efforts auto-PEEP raises tidal elastic pressure throughout inspiration, so it taxes the whole breath rather than only the gate. The lung and chest wall are also less compliant at high lung volumes: inward recoil of the chest cage adds to lung recoil at thoracic volumes greater than 60% of predicted vital capacity. Dynamic hyperinflation also compromises the efficiency of the inspiratory pump, because muscle fibres foreshorten and the inspiratory geometry becomes less conducive to force generation Marini 2011, PMID 21700908.

Three loads, and EPAP reaches only the first: a toll before flow, paid on every breath including the ones that fail to trigger; a stiffer system for the whole breath; and a worse pump, whose foreshortened fibres and flattened geometry mean the same neural drive produces less pressure. EPAP cannot touch the pump, which is the ceiling on what noninvasive pressure can do.

As far as this has been measured in a child: in ten infants with severe hypercapnic viral bronchiolitis, median intrinsic PEEP during spontaneous breathing was 6 cmH2O with a range of 3.9 to 9.2 Essouri 2011. That is bronchiolitis, not asthma. No equivalent series exists in pediatric asthma.

B4The picture

The Campbell diagram

The Campbell diagram partitions the work of breathing into elastic and resistive components, separates the lung from the chest wall, and is where intrinsic PEEP becomes visible as a threshold load rather than a number. It is the accepted standard for assessing work of breathing Respir Care 2026, PMID 42412522. Integrating the esophageal pressure–volume loop alone underestimates the work relative to the Campbell diagram and for that reason should not be used Banner 1994; the loop leaves out the chest wall, and the Campbell diagram puts it back by drawing the chest-wall relaxation line and measuring everything from it.

  1. The chest-wall relaxation line. At any volume this line marks where pleural pressure would sit if the inspiratory muscles were completely relaxed, so the horizontal distance between it and the actual pleural pressure trace is Pmus, read straight off the x axis. That conversion of a pressure tracing into a muscle-effort measurement is why the diagram is worth learning.
  2. The lung compliance line. Rising up and to the left from the same origin, its slope set by lung compliance. The area it encloses against the chest-wall line is the elastic work of the breath.
  3. The dynamic inspiratory limb. It bows left of the lung compliance line, and the width of that bow at any volume is the resistive pressure; the lens between the two is the resistive work. Airflow obstruction widens the lens, whereas reduced compliance grows the elastic area and flattens the loop. The lens is not the largest area at the default settings: elastic and resistive land within a hundredth of each other, and the biggest area is the threshold load, a toll paid before any gas moves rather than work of breathing in the usual sense. That is why EPAP is the first lever in B5 rather than IPAP.
  4. The threshold segment. A horizontal run at the bottom, at constant volume, whose length is intrinsic PEEP minus whatever EPAP has already paid. Pressure falls, volume does not move.

The colours follow Hess's convention: elastic work is the green area, resistive work the blue area, and the total shaded area is total work of breathing Hess 2014.

B5EPAP

What EPAP and CPAP do

EPAP pays off the toll, and the claim should be stated that narrowly. The pediatric statement: in patients with critical asthma, the occurrence of intrinsic PEEP increases the amount of muscle work needed to initiate airflow, and the application of positive expiratory pressure (through either noninvasive ventilation or CPAP) can offset the intrinsic PEEP, thus reducing inspiratory work and improving triggering Miller 2025.

The mechanism experiments are adult COPD and are named as such rather than blurred into the pediatric claim. In seven adults with severe COPD weaning from ventilation, CPAP at 5, 10 and 15 cmH2O produced progressive falls in inspiratory work, reaching 49.8% per minute and 41.8% per litre at the highest level, with the pressure–time product for the inspiratory muscles and the diaphragm falling 42.9% and 42.2% respectively. Tidal volume did not change. Inspiratory pulmonary resistance and elastance were unaltered. End-expiratory lung volume remained stable at the lowest level of CPAP, with only modest increases at the higher levels Petrof 1990. The work fell by 42% while the mechanics did not change; a toll got paid.

Adding PEEP also improved effective triggering sensitivity, diminished ventilatory drive and reduced the mechanical work of breathing while the end-expiratory alveolar-to-airway gradient narrowed; that study carried its own paralyzed control arm Smith 1988. B6IPAP

What IPAP does

IPAP is a different lever on a different load: EPAP pays the toll at the gate, IPAP part of the bill for the breath itself. In general, the greater the inspiratory pressure during noninvasive ventilation, the more muscle offloading will occur Miller 2025; raising Pvent during inspiration lets Pmus fall for the same elastic and resistive load. On the Campbell diagram the dynamic inspiratory limb slides back toward the chest-wall line and both muscular areas shrink, whereas EPAP shrinks the threshold area alone.

Noninvasive ventilation also augments tidal volume and increases the cross-sectional area of small airways throughout the respiratory cycle. The airway-calibre claim has one piece of asthma-population evidence behind it: in a randomized crossover study of asthmatic adolescents and young adults, assessed by optoelectronic plethysmography and spirometry after bronchoprovocation, bilevel noninvasive ventilation altered lung function and respiratory mechanics, the effect Miller describes as a bronchodilating effect independent of inhaled bronchodilators, presumably from airway stenting or a reduction in respiratory rate and gas trapping Gomes 2021.

The counterweight. Unloading the muscles is not the same as the patient doing less neural work. With inspiratory unloading of roughly 50% of resistance and elastance by proportional assist ventilation in 13 awake normal subjects, Pmus computed from esophageal pressure using the Campbell diagram, unloading raised ventilation and tidal volume at all levels of end-tidal CO2; with chemical stimuli rigorously controlled it did not downregulate respiratory muscle activation Am J Respir Crit Care Med 1997, PMID 9196108. The machine doing more does not mean the child is doing less. Drive is set by chemistry, so support that lowers CO2 lowers effort partly through that route rather than through mechanics alone.

The pediatric guardrail on IPAP is a volume rather than a comfort judgement: inspiratory pressure should be titrated to reduce work of breathing while maintaining tidal volume at or below 8 mL/kg, and if tidal volume exceeds 8 mL/kg, work of breathing should be reassessed before reducing inspiratory support Miller 2025.

B7Addition

Pressure support and PEEP, added together

One adult study measured the two loads separately and then together. B9High flow

High-flow nasal cannula versus CPAP

High flow is easy to reach for, and it is then often reasoned about as though it were low-dose CPAP. In obstructive disease that reasoning is wrong. Miller and Rotta attribute its benefit, in order, to washout of anatomical dead space, conditioning of inspired gas, overcoming nasopharyngeal resistance, and possibly a very small amount of PEEP (under 1 cmH2O) delivered to the intrathoracic airway Miller 2025. The qualifier carries the claim. The figure is for pressure reaching the intrathoracic airway, not for pressure measured in the nasopharynx, where studies at clinical flows report considerably higher values; a nasopharyngeal number quoted as though it were alveolar is the commonest way this device gets mistaken for low-dose CPAP. No nasopharyngeal-pressure figure is printed because none was sourced in this build unsourced.

The measurement behind that ordering: in 22 children three years or younger with bronchiolitis, flow titrated between 0.5 and 2 L/kg/min with electrical impedance tomography and esophageal manometry in a subset of 10, end-expiratory lung impedance rose by a median corresponding to 1.8 mL/kg of end-expiratory lung volume, although seven patients increased, twelve did not change and three decreased. Tidal impedance did not change. The pressure–rate product fell by a median of 78 cmH2O/min with every patient improving, while the change in esophageal pressure swing was not significant. The authors concluded that PEEP application is not the primary mechanism of action of high-flow nasal cannula in children with bronchiolitis Guglielmo 2022, PMID 35305971.

The consequence for a threshold load: if high flow does not reliably deliver end-expiratory pressure, it cannot reliably offset intrinsic PEEP. Whatever it is doing for a wheezing child, it is not what B5 describes.

Part C

Invasive ventilation and intrinsic PEEP

C1Inversion

Asthma versus ARDS: near-normal compliance, long time constants

Reflexes trained on ARDS point the wrong way here. In ARDS the lung is stiff, the time constants are short and PEEP is the therapy; in severe asthma the parenchyma is close to normal and the airway is the lesion, so the time constant is long, the lung does not finish emptying, and PEEP is the thing most likely to hurt you.

That inversion is stated directly in the pediatric literature: in asthma the lung parenchyma is essentially healthy with near-normal compliance, the problem is airway obstruction causing markedly elevated resistance, and dynamic hyperinflation becomes the primary threat, unlike ARDS, a disease of short time constants and decreased compliance Xiang 2026, PMID 42449090. Because compliance is near normal, a rising plateau pressure in asthma is almost always trapped gas rather than stiff lung, which is what the rest of Part C monitors: patients with severe air flow obstruction in asthma typically have near-normal respiratory system compliance, therefore an increase in plateau pressure usually reflects dynamic hyperinflation Newth 2025, PMID 40028856.

τ equals resistance times compliance, and a passively emptying lung unit gives back 63% of its volume in one time constant, 87% in two, 95% in three, 98% in four and 99% in five. Raise resistance five-fold at unchanged compliance and every milestone moves five times further out in time. Nothing about the alveolus has changed; the clock has.

The strategy that follows was published in 1984 and has not been improved on. Darioli and Perret ventilated 26 adults over 34 episodes of status asthmaticus by correcting hypoxemia with a hyperoxic mixture and deliberately not restoring alveolar ventilation, on the reasoning that high airway pressures appear to be more dangerous than persistent hypercapnia itself. All 26 survived; mean duration of controlled ventilation was 2.5 days. Ten were comatose and five in respiratory arrest at intubation. Controlled hypoventilation is the treatment rather than a compromise. Darioli 1984, PMID 6703497

C2Two patients

PEEP differs between the paralyzed and the spontaneously breathing patient

Almost every disagreement about PEEP in asthma dissolves once you ask whether the patient is making an inspiratory effort. The two states are two different problems rather than two settings of one.

The paralyzed patient has no trigger work to offload. Every documented benefit of applied PEEP in obstruction (improved triggering sensitivity, reduced ventilatory drive, reduced work of breathing) is a benefit to a breathing patient Smith 1988. Under paralysis that side of the ledger is zero and only the risk side remains. Tuxen paralyzed six adults with severe airflow obstruction, four asthma and two COPD, at fixed tidal volume 1.0 L and rates of 10, 16 and 22, and stepped PEEP through 5, 10 and 15 cmH2O. Functional residual capacity rose progressively, to 1.42 ± 0.43 L above baseline at PEEP 15, and two of the six could not be studied at PEEP 15 because they became hypotensive Tuxen 1989. That is the basis for teaching PEEP 0 to 5 in the passive asthmatic, and the pediatric literature repeats it: in paralyzed patients with near-fatal asthma, any PEEP will contribute to air trapping and hyperinflation Xiang 2026.

The spontaneous patient has the opposite problem. Trapped gas holds alveolar pressure above the circuit at end-expiration, so before any flow can occur the patient must generate an effort equal to intrinsic PEEP simply to reach zero. That threshold load is paid on every breath, including the ones that fail to trigger, and applied PEEP raises the circuit baseline toward alveolar pressure and shrinks the gap. Smith and Marini studied the same ten adults both assisted and paralyzed, and reported that adding PEEP improved the effective triggering sensitivity of the ventilator, diminished ventilatory drive, and reduced the mechanical work of breathing Smith 1988.

It is a small case series and not a trial, although it shows that the paralyzed strategy answers a particular problem rather than stating a law of asthma.

C3The number

Measuring intrinsic PEEP: sources of error

Intrinsic PEEP is the alveolar pressure that has not yet returned to baseline when the next breath begins. It is measured by occluding the airway at end-expiration and reading the pressure above set PEEP Hess 2014. The hold length and the settling time have to be stated together, or the number is not interpretable. The conventional hold is 0.5 to 2 seconds and a stable value needs 2 to 3 seconds even with every airway open, so a hold under about 2 seconds underreads in the best case and badly once dependent airways close early, which is point 3 below. In severe pediatric cases it typically runs 5 to 20 cmH2O, and a value above 10 is taken as severe air trapping requiring a change in strategy Xiang 2026, although the citation behind that range is adult, so it is a review's stated range rather than a pediatric measurement.

The measurement fails in four distinct ways, and three of the four fail specifically in asthma.

  1. The patient must be passive. A valid measurement needs a relaxed patient breathing in synchrony with the ventilator; active breathing invalidates it, so in a child who is triggering an expiratory-hold number is not an intrinsic PEEP. That alone undoes the 80%-of-intrinsic-PEEP rule in the population it is most often invoked for, because the denominator cannot be obtained in the patient it is meant for.
  2. Airway closure hides gas from the manometer. The occlusion reads a volume-weighted average of only those units still communicating with the central airway, so units that closed before end-expiration hold higher pressures that never reach the sensor and measured auto-PEEP may dramatically underestimate dynamic hyperinflation and barotrauma risk in asthma Marini 2011. Leatherman documented it in four ventilated asthmatics: measured auto-PEEP 5 ± 3 cmH2O against a peak of 76 ± 7 and a plateau of 28 ± 2. In three of them, prolonging expiratory time dropped measured auto-PEEP from 14 ± 4 to 5 ± 4 while the plateau barely moved, 31 ± 3 to 29 ± 3. The manometer improved. The patient did not. Leatherman 1996, PMID 8625647
  3. The wave keeps rising. With all airways open a stable value is reached within 2 to 3 seconds. With extensive dependent airway closure the trapped gas decompresses slowly into the central airway and pressure keeps rising for many seconds, so a two-second hold that then releases will underread that patient.
  4. Expiratory muscle activity inflates it. In an actively expiring patient the esophageal-derived intrinsic PEEP overestimates the true threshold load unless corrected for the gastric pressure decay. The number can therefore be falsely low from airway closure and falsely high from abdominal effort, sometimes in the same patient on the same day.

Intrinsic PEEP is worth measuring for the direction of travel and is a poor denominator for arithmetic. Marini says the same of the plateau: for a fixed tidal volume, end-inspiratory plateau pressure is a better guide than the measurable auto-PEEP to the severity of dynamic hyperinflation and to treatment response. The rest of Part C rests on that.

C4Plateau

Plateau is the hyperinflation measure. Peak is the tube plus the airway.

Airway pressure during a constant-flow breath decomposes cleanly. Peak minus plateau is set by resistance and flow; plateau minus the total PEEP the lung actually sits at is set by tidal volume and respiratory system compliance. As bedside arithmetic, inspiratory resistance equals peak minus plateau divided by inspiratory flow, and expiratory resistance equals plateau minus total PEEP divided by peak expiratory flow. Total, not set. Using set PEEP inflates the numerator by the whole of the auto-PEEP in every patient this part describes, which is why the stacked bar below draws set PEEP and auto-PEEP as separate segments. For an intubated patient inspiratory resistance should be under 10 cmH2O/L/s, and expiratory resistance is typically higher Hess 2014 adult.

That threshold is adult and does not transfer to a small child, because the number is measured through the tube. In 21 ventilated children the peak measured at the ventilator was 26 ± 8 cmH2O against 19 ± 7 at the tracheal tip Pediatr Crit Care Med 2005, PMID 15730605, and on the bench the drop across a 2.0 mm tube exceeded 8 mbar with tube size the dominant determinant Physiol Meas 2011, PMID 21799238. A small child with healthy lungs can clear 10 cmH2O/L/s on tube resistance alone: a normal airway through a narrow tube, not severe obstruction. No pediatric replacement threshold was found in this build unsourced, so none is printed.

The peak carries the resistive term and the plateau does not. In asthma the resistive term is large: peak 76 ± 7 against plateau 28 ± 2 in one series, a resistive drop of roughly 48 cmH2O Leatherman 1996, so the peak is dominated by something that is not distending the alveolus. Tuxen and Lane showed this in nine paralyzed adults with severe airflow obstruction, five asthma and four chronic airflow obstruction, stepping minute ventilation through 10, 16 and 26 L/min and inspiratory flow through 40, 70 and 100 L/min while measuring end-inspiratory lung volume by prolonged apnea: peak airway pressure was predominantly related to inspiratory flow and did not reflect changes in lung volume Tuxen 1987, PMID 3662241. Hyperinflation reached 3.6 ± 0.4 L above functional residual capacity at the highest settings, and the ventilation required for normocapnia before paralysis, 15.7 ± 2.3 L/min, produced hypotension in seven of nine.

Hyperinflation rather than pressure is what hurts the patient. Williams and Tuxen followed 22 ventilated asthmatics prospectively: end-inspiratory lung volume was 26.1 ± 4.7 mL/kg in those who developed complications against 20.0 ± 7.4 mL/kg in those who did not, and only that volume had a threshold predictive of harm: below 1.4 L, 0 of 5 had complications; at or above 1.4 L, 11 of 17 did (65%, p = 0.03). Barotrauma occurred in 27% and hypotension in 41% Williams 1992, PMID 1519836. Marini generalises it: it is not total PEEP that risks overdistention, but rather the associated end-inspiratory alveolar pressure.

C6Settings

Minute ventilation is the primary driver of hyperinflation

Under passive conditions the variables that drive hyperinflation are airway resistance, a long inspiratory duty cycle, and minute ventilation; perhaps the most effective strategy is to reduce ventilation Marini 2011. Auto-PEEP falls when you cut minute ventilation by rate or tidal volume, lengthen expiratory time by cutting rate or inspiratory time, or cut resistance with a bronchodilator Hess 2014. Three things to flag rather than smooth over. The pediatric tidal-volume recommendation contradicts itself inside one review. Rate 6–12 is written without reference to age: in a two-year-old whose resting rate is near 30, a set rate of 8 is a far more extreme intervention than the same number in an adolescent, and no source found here scales it by age unsourced. On mode, the pediatric review argues that pressure control distributes gas more evenly across heterogeneous time constants, while Marini points the other way: under pressure targeting, adding PEEP below auto-PEEP raises the effective driving pressure and the delivered volume, and past that point further PEEP stops raising volume and may reduce it as the lung stiffens. In volume control the same manoeuvre shows up as a plateau that stays flat and then rises in proportion to the PEEP increment. Neither mode is wrong; they fail differently, so watch the plateau in volume control and the delivered tidal volume in pressure control.

Permissive hypercapnia. PaCO2 of 80 to 100 mmHg is commonly tolerated and pH above 7.20 is generally safe in the absence of other complications, and the two that matter here are named rather than gestured at. Hypercapnia raises cerebral blood flow and cerebral blood volume, so it is not benign in raised intracranial pressure or acute brain injury; hypercapnia with acidaemia raises pulmonary vascular resistance, so it is not benign in pre-existing pulmonary hypertension or right-ventricular dysfunction. Both are physiologic rationale rather than a graded recommendation, and neither is a threshold. Bicarbonate is rarely indicated and is potentially harmful because it adds CO2 to a patient who cannot clear it, and should be considered only below pH 7.15 with hemodynamic instability, given slowly at 0.5–1 mEq/kg over 30–60 minutes Xiang 2026. And the reading that matters most in the child who is still breathing: a PaCO2 of 40 mmHg in a tachypneic child with critical asthma is impending respiratory failure, not reassurance.

C7PEEP, passive

PEEP in the passive, paralyzed patient

Start with the passive patient, because that is who you have at the moment of intubation. Set PEEP at 0 to 5 cmH2O and leave it there. That is the setting rather than the first rung of a ladder. This page does not teach routine stepwise up-titration of PEEP in the paralyzed asthmatic, and one widely read source teaches the opposite: EMCrit's Internet Book of Critical Care titrates the passive patient up to about 75% of intrinsic PEEP and holds at 5 cmH2O in the triggering patient, which inverts the order used here EMCrit IBCC. That version has support in Caramez, whose whole cohort was paralyzed and in whom a monitored PEEP trial was the explicit recommendation; the version here has support in Tuxen's paralyzed asthmatics and in the trigger-work literature, which by construction applies only to a breathing patient, and it is the order Deranged Physiology teaches. What is not defensible in either state is dialling in 75% or 80% of a measured intrinsic PEEP and walking away. Where a higher level is tried it is a monitored trial with an explicit end, not a standing order: small steps, attending present, and two stop rules armed before the first step. Return to the previous level if the plateau rises, and return to the previous level if the blood pressure falls. The second rule is not redundant: two of Tuxen's six paralyzed patients could not be studied at PEEP 15 at all because they became hypotensive Tuxen 1989, and in Ranieri's nine paralyzed adults PEEP beyond the critical pressure reduced cardiac index and compromised gas exchange Ranieri 1993. Neither paper establishes that the plateau always moves first, which is why both limits are watched rather than one. Recheck as the child improves, because the intrinsic PEEP being titrated against falls as the bronchospasm resolves: a set PEEP that sat safely below it on day one can exceed it on day three with nobody touching the dial.

Every clause of that has a source. Initial PEEP 0–5 cmH2O Stather 2005, PMID 16356242; zero or minimal PEEP at or under 4 cmH2O during controlled ventilation with paralysis, starting at zero immediately after intubation Xiang 2026; PEEP titration should be immediately stopped if plateau increases Demoule 2020, PMID 33169215; and the endpoint stated as a target rather than a rule: an external-PEEP trial to determine the level resulting in the minimum plateau pressure might be a useful bedside approach, with acceptable drawbacks Caramez 2005, PMID 16003057.

The safety envelope is real and it is narrow. In nine paralyzed COPD adults with flow limitation and intrinsic PEEP of 9.8 ± 0.5 cmH2O, applied PEEP of 5 and 10 changed neither lung volume, nor total PEEP, nor hemodynamics, nor gas exchange. Past that, lung volume rose, elastance of both lung and chest wall rose, and cardiac index fell: PEEP levels exceeding 85% of intrinsic PEEP caused further hyperinflation and compromised hemodynamics and gas exchange Ranieri 1993, PMID 8420430.

The envelope is not the same in asthma. Marini notes that tidal flow limitation is more likely to occur during an exacerbation of COPD than an acute asthmatic attack, and flow limitation is precisely the condition under which PEEP below the critical pressure is free. Where there is no flow limitation, adding PEEP to lungs without it simply distends them further. Tuxen's paralyzed cohort was two-thirds asthma and PEEP raised functional residual capacity at every level and every rate.

The plateau is the endpoint rather than a preset number because, under flow-limited volume-cycled ventilation, adding PEEP to the airway exerts little impact on the plateau pressure until auto-PEEP is nearly counterbalanced; beyond that level, adding PEEP while maintaining tidal volume causes plateau pressure to rise in proportion to the PEEP increment. The plateau is quiet while you are safe and starts moving the moment you are not.

PEEP-response phenotypes in obstructed adults

Caramez applied external PEEP in 2 cmH2O steps every five minutes, from zero to 150% of each patient's baseline intrinsic PEEP, in eight paralyzed adults with severe obstruction (four asthma, four COPD; baseline intrinsic PEEP 5.0 to 13.5 cmH2O; inspiratory resistance above 20 cmH2O/L/s), repeating the ladder at four ventilatory patterns Caramez 2005, PMID 16003057.

Three responses appeared. In the biphasic pattern, functional residual capacity, plateau pressure and isovolume expiratory flow stayed flat as PEEP climbed until a critical pressure was exceeded, past which volume and plateau rose sharply and flow fell. In classic overinflation, any PEEP increment raised volume and plateau and cut expiratory flow. In the paradoxical response, volume, total PEEP and plateau all fell while isovolume expiratory flow rose; in one patient applied PEEP produced a 450 mL fall in functional residual capacity and a 7 cmH2O fall in plateau pressure.

Across at least one ventilatory pattern, the paradoxical response appeared in five of eight patients (three asthma, two COPD), the biphasic in four of eight (two and two), and the classic overinflation in six of eight (three and three). Those counts sum to fifteen across eight patients because the authors observed a variable behavior, deflation versus inflation, depending on tidal volume, respiratory rate and external-PEEP settings, which could not be related to baseline disease or mechanics. The same patient can be a paradoxical responder at one tidal volume and an overinflator at another. That is a state of a patient at a setting rather than a patient phenotype.

C9PEEP, spontaneous

PEEP in the spontaneously breathing patient

Once the child is breathing, the balance flips: there is now a threshold load to remove, and applied PEEP removes it. The pediatric statement of the rule: during assisted ventilation with spontaneous breathing effort, the application of PEEP up to approximately 80% of measured intrinsic PEEP can reduce triggering work and improve synchrony without additionally increasing lung volume Xiang 2026. In practice: start at zero after intubation, then once resistance has improved and blockade has stopped, titrate PEEP carefully to help synchrony while watching for worsening hyperinflation. Newth and Ross put the same idea in one clause: intrinsic PEEP is worth measuring precisely because the PEEP set on the ventilator can be lower than it Newth 2025.

Internal inconsistency. The same pediatric paper's weaning section says applying PEEP to match intrinsic PEEP improves triggering: 100%, not 80%, in the same review. So 75, 80, 85 and 100 percent are all in print, and the trail runs back to a single physiologic study of nine paralyzed adults with COPD in 1993.

The denominator problem

To take 80% of intrinsic PEEP you must know intrinsic PEEP, and the expiratory-hold measurement is invalid in an actively breathing patient, which is the only patient the rule applies to. In asthma the measurement can also be falsely low, because closed airways hide their pressure from the manometer. Eighty percent of a number that cannot legitimately be measured in this patient, and that under-reads when it can, is a heuristic rather than a calculation.

Titrate to the effect rather than to the arithmetic. Add PEEP in small steps while watching the trigger: missed efforts should disappear and the flow trace should stop showing efforts that produce no breath. Watch the plateau at the same time, and if it rises you have gone past. Hess makes the diagnostic explicit: the presence of missed triggers and flow limitation suggests that PEEP might effectively counterbalance auto-PEEP, so the patient's own waveform rather than a percentage says who will benefit. The evidence that the benefit is real is all adult: in ten ventilated adults with airflow obstruction studied both assisted and paralyzed, PEEP narrowed the end-expiratory gap between alveolar and central airway pressure, improved effective triggering sensitivity, reduced ventilatory drive and reduced the work of breathing without substantially increasing the hazards of hyperinflation Smith 1988.

C12Waveform

Reading the expiratory flow trace

The flow-time curve is the cheapest hyperinflation monitor on the ventilator and is free on every breath. End-expiratory flow is present if resistance is high and expiratory time is not sufficient, indicating air trapping Hess 2014. Stated for the bedside in the pediatric review: failure of expiratory flow to return to zero before the next breath confirms air trapping, and the shape of the expiratory limb, scooped versus linear, tracks the severity of obstruction Xiang 2026. Demoule frames it as a prompt: dynamic hyperinflation and intrinsic PEEP should be considered whenever expiratory flow does not cease at end-expiration Demoule 2020.

What the flow magnitude does and does not tell you. It says that trapping is happening, not how much pressure is behind it: the magnitude of flow bears little relation to the level of auto-PEEP that drives it through an undetermined upstream resistance Marini 2011. Treat end-expiratory flow as a yes-or-no plus a direction of travel and take the amount from the plateau. The trend is what it gives you: in the Leatherman cohort, end-expiratory flow fell from 61.4 ± 12.6 to 38.6 ± 4.5 to 23.1 ± 5.8 mL/s as the rate came down from 18 to 12 to 6. A flow trace approaching zero as you intervene is the same information as a falling plateau, arriving one breath at a time rather than one hold at a time.

C13Crashing

Hyperinflation as a cause of post-intubation hypotension

Hypotension after intubation in severe asthma is close to universal, and only part of it is the sedative. It follows from sedation and from the loss of the markedly negative inspiratory pleural pressures the patient had been generating, although dynamic hyperinflation is itself a major contributor: it raises pulmonary vascular resistance and right ventricular afterload, and the auto-PEEP raises pleural and right atrial pressure, impeding venous return. Transmission is worse when the lung is normal: the fraction of alveolar pressure reaching the pleural space is set by the ratio of chest wall elastance to total respiratory system elastance, so pulmonary vascular, pleural and right atrial pressures rise more when the lungs are flexible than when they are not Marini 2011. The near-normal compliance that makes asthma feel reassuring on the plateau is exactly what makes hyperinflation hemodynamically dangerous.

The most dangerous minute is the one after the tube goes in, before anyone has set a ventilator. Over-enthusiastic bagging at an ordinary rate produces rapid hyperinflation with severe hemodynamic consequences, and severe hyperinflation can abolish the end-tidal CO2 trace through dead space, which is then misread as a dislodged tube Xiang 2026. During resuscitation of an obstructed patient, auto-PEEP generated by aggressive bagging can impair ventricular loading and produce pulseless electrical activity that reverses when ventilation is reduced.

The manoeuvre: disconnect and wait. In an obstructed adult with electromechanical dissociation after intubation, spontaneous respirations and blood pressure returned fifteen minutes after resuscitation was stopped, and the authors recommend a brief trial of apnea for patients with COPD and electromechanical dissociation when conventional measures are unsuccessful Rogers 1991, PMID 1989814. That is a single adult case report and is presented as one. It is taught for the cost of not thinking of it rather than the strength of the evidence, and the mechanism is stated independently by Marini.

Barotrauma. In mechanically ventilated children with asthma, barotrauma occurs in approximately 6%, with named risk factors of severe dynamic hyperinflation, plateau above 30 cmH2O, high minute ventilation, patient-ventilator asynchrony, and ventilation beyond 72 hours Xiang 2026. In the CPCCRN pediatric cohort, barotrauma had already occurred before PICU admission in 15 of 261 children (6%). In adult asthma the rates run higher: 27% barotrauma and 41% hypotension in one prospective series Williams 1992.

Outcome, for calibration. Immediate mortality in pediatric asthma is near zero for children who reach care without a preceding arrest. Across eight CPCCRN PICUs, 11 of 261 ventilated children died (4%), and 10 of the 11 had arrested before admission; 96% survived to discharge J Pediatr 2012. Newth and Ross add the tail: most children who die arrest before hospitalization, although there is a suggestion of additional mortality from asthma in the following decade. Data from Melbourne, cited secondhand in the pediatric review, put that at 5% mortality within 10 years of a PICU admission, with up to 88% having persistent symptoms. Surviving the intubation is not the end of the risk. For scale on how rarely this content now applies: across 67,614 PICU admissions for asthma in US children between 2009 and 2019, intubation fell from 6.9% to 3.4% while noninvasive ventilation as maximal support rose from 8.9% to 20.0%, both p < 0.001 Smith 2023, PMID 36315585, which is why this has to be taught rather than learned by repetition.
